01 What is “Enterprise Structure”?

The first configuration topic in every SAP FI project — and in the C_TS4FI exam.

Think about how a real business is organised. A business group may own several legal companies. Each legal company may run several factories and warehouses. It sells through sales offices, buys through a purchasing department, and keeps accounts for the tax office and the law.

SAP cannot work with a vague idea like “our business”. SAP needs this structure drawn exactly, piece by piece, inside the system. That drawing — the complete map of how your business is built inside SAP — is called the Enterprise Structure.

New term · Enterprise Structure

Enterprise Structure = the framework of organisational units (Client, Company, Company Code, Plant, Sales Organisation and so on) that represents your real business structure inside SAP. Every transaction you ever post must belong to one of these units.

Enterprise Structure matlab: aapke asal business ka SAP ke andar banaya gaya naqsha — kaunsi legal company, kaunsi factory, kaunsa office.

New term · Organisational Unit (Org Unit)

An organisational unit is one building block of that map — one box in the diagram. “Company Code”, “Plant” and “Sales Organisation” are all organisational units. You will also see the short form org unit.

Analogy · The family tree / organogram

Every company has an organogram — the chart on the HR wall showing the CEO at the top, departments below, teams below them. The Enterprise Structure is exactly that chart, but drawn inside SAP, and with one strict rule: every rupee, every material and every employee must sit inside one box of this chart. Nothing floats outside it.

Jaise daftar ki deewaar par laga hua chart — bara sahab upar, phir departments, phir teams. SAP mein bhi har cheez ko aik box mein hona lazmi hai.

Why must this come FIRST?

  • Every posting needs an address. When you post a journal entry in SAP, the system asks: which company code? If no company code exists, there is nowhere for the entry to go.
  • Legal reports come from this structure. A Balance Sheet is prepared per company code. Wrong structure = wrong legal reports.
  • It is very hard to change later. Once thousands of documents are posted, you cannot simply rename or merge company codes. Consultants design this structure carefully at the start of the project.
Memory trick

“Pehle makaan ka naqsha, phir furniture.” First the house plan (enterprise structure), then the furniture (accounts, postings, reports). In every SAP project and in this course, structure always comes before transactions.

02 The full map — all org units in one picture

Do not memorise yet. Just see the shape of the whole thing; we will zoom into each box one by one.

How to read this map

  • Top to bottom = big to small. Client is the biggest container; a storage location is one of the smallest.
  • One box can hold many boxes below it. One client → many companies → many company codes → many plants.
  • Every box has a code. SAP identifies each unit by a short code — 1000, 1100, ABCGRP. You will type these codes all day in SAP.
  • Different modules own different boxes. FI owns the Company Code. MM (Materials Management — purchasing and stock) owns Plant and Storage Location. SD (Sales & Distribution — selling) owns the Sales Organisation. CO (Controlling — internal cost reporting) owns the Controlling Area.
New terms · Module names you just met

FI = Financial Accounting (legal books). CO = Controlling (internal costs). MM = Materials Management (purchase + stock). SD = Sales & Distribution (selling + billing). These short names are used everywhere in SAP — learn them now.

04 Client — the top floor

You met the Client in Lesson 2 (Architecture). Here is its place in the structure — short, because the exam only needs the essentials.

Quick recap · Client

A client is a self-contained environment inside one SAP system, identified by a 3-digit number (000, 001, 100…). Data of one client is separate from another client, like separate tenants in one building who cannot see each other’s rooms. Standard SAP clients: 000 (SAP reference), 001 (copy of 000), 066 (SAP EarlyWatch service).

  • The client sits above everything in the enterprise structure — company codes, plants, all of them are created inside a client.
  • Enterprise structure settings are client-dependent: a company code you create in client 100 does not exist in client 200 of the same system. It must be transported or created there.
  • One client normally represents one business group in the productive system.
Memory trick

Client = Building, Company Code = Flat. Many flats (company codes) in one building (client) — each flat has its own lock, its own family, its own accounts.

05 Company — the group / holding level

Video 01:34:50 onwards — the first org unit the course defines.

Many businesses are not one company — they are a group. A holding company owns several separate legal companies: one in Pakistan, one in the UAE, one in the UK. Each of those is legally independent, files its own taxes, and has its own registration.

But the owners also want one combined report for the whole group: “What is our total group profit?” In accounting, combining the books of all group companies into one set of group statements is called consolidation.

New term · Consolidation

Consolidation = merging the financial statements of all companies in a group into one group Balance Sheet and P&L, after removing (eliminating) transactions the group companies did with each other — because a group cannot make a profit by selling to itself.

Poore group ki books ko aik bana kar dikhana — aur group ke andar ki khareed-o-farokht ko khatam karna.

New term · Company (in SAP)

In SAP, a Company is the organisational unit that represents a group of company codes whose books must be consolidated together. Its ID can be up to 6 characters (letters and numbers). SAP also calls each company a trading partner, because group companies trade with each other and those internal trades must be identified for consolidation.

Key facts about Company

  • T-code to define: OX15 — or SPRO → Enterprise Structure → Definition → Financial Accounting → Define Company.
  • Stored in table T880.
  • Defining a company is optional if you never consolidate — a single-company business may skip it. But the moment a group wants consolidated statements, companies must be defined and company codes assigned to them.
  • When one group company sells to another, the posting carries the trading partner (the other company’s ID). At consolidation, SAP uses this to eliminate the internal sale and purchase against each other.
  • One company can have many company codes assigned to it — for example, if one legal group entity keeps separate books for two regions.
Analogy · The father and his sons’ shops

A father owns three shops, each registered separately in a son’s name (three legal entities = three company codes). For the bank and the law, each shop files separately. But at home, the father asks one question: “Poori family ne total kitna kamaya?” — that family-level view is the Company (group) level, and putting the three shops’ results together is consolidation.

06 Company Code — the heart of FI

Video 01:40:00 onwards. If you understand only one thing in this lesson, understand this.

You already know from Financial Accounting that every legal entity — a registered company — must keep its own complete books: its own Balance Sheet, its own Profit & Loss, its own tax filings. SAP needs one box for exactly that legal entity. That box is the Company Code.

New term · Company Code

A Company Code is the smallest organisational unit for which a complete, self-contained set of accounts can be drawn up — a full Balance Sheet and Profit & Loss statement for a legally independent entity. Its ID is exactly 4 characters (letters and/or numbers, e.g. 1000, PK01).

Company Code = aik mustaqil legal company jis ki apni mukammal Balance Sheet aur P&L banti hai. FI mein sab kuch isi ke gird ghoomta hai.

Why the company code is the centre of everything in FI

  • No company code = no posting. Every FI document (journal entry) carries a company code in its header. The field is mandatory — SAP will not save the document without it.
  • Legal reports are per company code. Balance Sheet, P&L, tax returns — all are produced company code by company code.
  • Everything else in FI hangs from it. Chart of accounts, fiscal year, currencies, document types, vendors, customers — in the coming lessons, almost every setting will be made for a company code.
  • One client, many company codes. A group with 12 legal companies creates 12 company codes inside one client.
  • Each company code has its own currency — the currency of its country (its local / company code currency), e.g. PKR for a Pakistani company code. Every posting in that company code is recorded in this currency.
New term · Local Currency (Company Code Currency)

The local currency is the currency in which a company code keeps its legal books — the currency of the country where that legal entity is registered. A Pakistani company code: PKR. A UAE company code: AED.

Analogy · Separate bahi-khata for each registered shop

In the bazaar, one family may run three separately-registered shops. Each shop keeps its own bahi-khata (ledger book), its own bank account, its own tax number. Money moving from Shop A to Shop B is recorded in both books. SAP does the same: each company code is a separate bahi-khata, and inter-company movements are recorded in both — with the trading partner noted, so the family (group) accounts can cancel the internal entries out.

Memory tricks · Company Code

“Code = Complete books.” Company Code → Complete set of accounts. And the size: Company Code = 4 characters, Company = 6 characters — the smaller unit has the smaller… no wait, remember it the exam way: “Code is short (4), Company is longer (6)” — the word “Company” itself is longer than the word “Code”.

The exam loves statements like “A company code can be assigned to multiple companies.” — False. A company code is assigned to one company only. It is the company that can collect many company codes. Direction matters: many → one.

08 Creating a Company Code, step by step

In a real project this is done in SPRO (the configuration menu you met in Lesson 3). Two routes exist — copy or create fresh.

Quick recap · SPRO & IMG

SPRO is the transaction that opens SAP’s configuration menu (the IMG — Implementation Guide). All enterprise-structure settings in this lesson live under: SPRO → Enterprise Structure → Definition / Assignment → Financial Accounting.

Route A — Copy an existing company code (the practical way)

SAP delivers ready-made sample company codes (for example a German and a US sample). Consultants usually copy one, because copying brings along hundreds of ready settings — tables, currencies, tax procedures — that would take days to build by hand.

  • T-code EC01 — Copy Company Code (copy organisational object).
  • Enter the source company code (the SAP sample) and your new 4-character code.
  • SAP asks whether to also copy the G/L accounts — decide carefully; copying accounts is convenient in a training system.
  • After copying, adjust the details (name, city, country, currency) with OX02.

Route B — Define a fresh company code

  • Step 1. Go to OX02 (SPRO → Enterprise Structure → Definition → Financial Accounting → Edit, Copy, Delete, Check Company Code).
  • Step 2. Choose the activity Edit Company Code Data and press New Entries.
  • Step 3. Type your 4-character code (e.g. 1000) and the company name.
  • Step 4. Fill the additional data: City, Country, Currency (the local currency of its books) and Language.
  • Step 5. Maintain the formal address (the “Edit Company Code Address” activity) — name, street, city, country — this address prints on official documents and invoices.
  • Step 6. Save. Your entries travel in a Customizing transport request (Lesson 2) from DEV to QAS to PRD.

Exam traps · Creating company codes

• A company code ID is 4 characters — it can contain letters, not only numbers (PK01 is valid).
• Deleting a company code is possible in a test system, but once productive postings exist, you do not delete it — real projects archive instead.
• The country you choose matters: it drives country-specific settings such as the tax procedure and, if used, a country-specific chart of accounts (both in later lessons).

09 Assignments — joining the pieces

Defining boxes is only half the work. The other half is telling SAP how the boxes connect. These connections are called assignments.

New term · Assignment

An assignment is a link between two org units — for example, “Company Code 1000 belongs to Company ABCGRP”. In SPRO, all assignments live under Enterprise Structure → Assignment. Rule of thumb: Definition creates the box; Assignment connects the box.

Definition = dabba banao. Assignment = dabbon ko aapas mein joro.

AssignmentRuleT-code / where
Company Code → CompanyEach company code → one company; one company ← many company codesOX16
Company Code → Controlling AreaEach company code → one controlling area; one controlling area ← many company codesIn controlling area maintenance, OX06
Plant → Company CodeEach plant → exactly one company code; one company code ← many plantsOX18
Storage Location → PlantEach storage location belongs to one plantDefined within the plant, OX09 area of SPRO
Sales Organisation → Company CodeEach sales org → one company codeSPRO → Enterprise Structure → Assignment → Sales and Distribution
Company Code → Credit Control AreaA company code can be controlled by one credit control areaOB38 (credit control area itself: OB45)

The controlling area rule — read it twice Exam favourite

One controlling area can serve several company codes — this is called cross-company-code cost accounting, and it lets management see costs for the whole group in one place. But it is allowed only if all those company codes:

  • use the same chart of accounts, and
  • use the same fiscal year variant (fiscal year = the 12-month accounting year; full detail in Lesson 7).

If either differs, those company codes need separate controlling areas. Aik controlling area, kai company codes — shart: chart of accounts aur fiscal year aik hi ho.

Analogy · Plug and socket

Defining org units is like buying appliances. Assignments are the plugs in the sockets. A plant that is not plugged into a company code is a fridge standing in the shop — it exists, but it cannot run: no stock valuation, no postings. SAP even refuses many transactions until the assignment exists.

The story. “ABC Group” is a Faisalabad-based textile group. It has a registered Pakistani company running a spinning mill in Faisalabad and a warehouse in Karachi, a trading company in Dubai, and a small sales company in London. Management wants group-level cost reporting across all three.

That one test — “does it need its own Balance Sheet?” — decides almost every structure question in the exam.

13 Exam traps

The mistakes C_TS4FI is designed to catch. Each one has appeared in practice questions in some form.

Trap 1 · “Company” prepares the Balance Sheet

No. The company code prepares the legal Balance Sheet and P&L. The company only receives consolidated group statements.

Trap 2 · Wrong ID lengths

Company code = 4 characters. Company = 6 characters. Client = 3 digits. Options in the exam swap these on purpose.

Trap 3 · Company is mandatory

No — defining a company is optional unless you need consolidation. The company code is the mandatory one.

Trap 4 · One company code → many companies

False. One company code is assigned to one company. One company collects many company codes.

Trap 5 · A plant belongs to many company codes

False. A plant is assigned to exactly one company code — that is how stock value lands in one legal entity’s books.

Trap 6 · Controlling area always 1-to-1

False. One controlling area can cover many company codes (cross-company-code cost accounting) — if they share the same chart of accounts and fiscal year variant.

Trap 7 · Org units are the same in every client

False. Enterprise structure is client-dependent — defined per client and moved between systems with Customizing transports.

Trap 8 · Every branch office needs a company code

False. Only an entity that needs its own complete legal financial statements gets a company code. A branch or warehouse inside the same legal entity is a plant (or, for internal segment reporting, a business area / profit center).

15 Summary — the whole lesson on one screen

  • Enterprise Structure = the map of your business inside SAP. It is built first, because every posting must belong to an org unit.
  • Top to bottom: Client → Company → Company Code → Plant → Storage Location, with the Controlling Area spanning company codes for CO.
  • Company = group level, 6 characters, optional, for consolidation; defined in OX15, table T880, also called the trading partner.
  • Company Code = one legal entity, 4 characters, mandatory, smallest unit with a complete Balance Sheet + P&L; defined in OX02 (copy with EC01), table T001, field BUKRS.
  • Assign company code → company in OX16. One code → one company; one company → many codes.
  • A plant belongs to exactly one company code (OX18). A controlling area can serve many company codes only if they share the same chart of accounts and fiscal year variant.
  • A company code becomes postable through its global parameters in OBY6: chart of accounts, fiscal year variant, field status variant, posting period variant.
  • The one test for any structure question: “Does it need its own Balance Sheet?” Yes → company code. No → plant / business area / profit center.

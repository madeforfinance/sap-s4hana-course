01 Where the course stands — from G/L to the vendor's ledger

You can post G/L entries. Now meet the part of the books where a company tracks what it owes its suppliers.

In Part 2 · Lesson 4 you posted entries directly between G/L accounts with FB50 and F-02. But think about a real business day at Ibrahim Apparel & Trading: cloth arrives from three mills, buttons from a trader in Karachi, rent is due, the electricity bill lands. Every one of those creates the same question — “how much do we owe this supplier, for which bill, and by when?” A single G/L account called “Payables” cannot answer that per supplier. So SAP keeps a detailed side-ledger for suppliers: Accounts Payable, shortened to AP.

Key term · Subledger

A subledger is a detailed side-book that sits under one G/L account. Accounts Payable is the subledger for suppliers (vendors); Accounts Receivable is the subledger for customers. The G/L shows only the total owed to all vendors; the AP subledger shows the detail — vendor by vendor, bill by bill. Subledger matlab tafseeli register: G/L mein sirf kul raqam, subledger mein har supplier ka alag hisaab.

Key term · Vendor

In SAP language, a supplier is called a vendor — any person or company you buy goods or services from and therefore owe money to: the cloth mill, the landlord, the courier company. One vendor = one master record + one account in the AP subledger. Vendor = supplier, jis se khareedari hoti hai aur jis ke paisay dene hote hain.

02 The vendor master & the Business Partner (BP)

Before any invoice can be booked, the vendor must exist in SAP. In S/4HANA, that existence is called a Business Partner.

You already know the G/L account master from FS00 (Lesson 4): no master, no posting. Vendors work the same way. A vendor master record holds everything SAP needs to know about a supplier once, so that every invoice and payment afterwards can simply use the vendor's number.

Key term · Business Partner (BP)

In S/4HANA, vendors and customers are no longer created as separate old-style masters. They are created as a Business Partner — one central record for a person or organisation — and then given roles that say what that partner does: a vendor role for suppliers, a customer role for buyers. The transaction is BP. The same company can be both your supplier and your customer — one BP, two roles. Business Partner aik hi central record hai; role batata hai ke ye supplier hai, customer hai, ya dono.

The vendor master has three layers

Just like the G/L master had a chart segment and a company code segment, the vendor master is built in layers. Each layer is maintained by a different team and used by different transactions:

Layer (segment)What it holdsWho uses it
General data (client level — valid everywhere)Name, address, phone, email, tax numbers (NTN), bank details of the vendorEveryone — entered once for the whole system
Company code data (FI view — per company code)Reconciliation account, payment terms, payment methods, house bank hints, tolerance groupFinance (FI) — invoices FB60, payments F-53 / F110
Purchasing data (per purchasing organisation)Order currency, purchasing terms, the buyer-side settingsPurchasing (MM) — purchase orders and MIRO

In BP, these layers appear as roles. The two roles you must know for this lesson:

  • FLVN00 — Vendor (FI): gives the partner its company code data — this is the role that makes it a vendor for accounting.
  • FLVN01 — Vendor (Purchasing): gives the partner its purchasing organisation data — needed when purchase orders are used.
Key term · Role (BP role)

A role is a “hat” a Business Partner wears. Adding a role adds the screens and data for that job. FLVN00 is the accounting hat for a vendor; FLVN01 is the purchasing hat. Role matlab topi — aik hi shakhs (BP) mukhtalif topiyan pehen sakta hai.

Analogy · CNIC wala record

Business Partner ko NADRA ke record ki tarah samjhein: aik shakhs, aik CNIC number. Phir wohi shakhs driver bhi ban sakta hai (driving licence role), passport bhi le sakta hai (passport role) — bunyadi record aik hi rehta hai, roles badalte rehte hain. SAP mein BP wohi CNIC record hai; FLVN00 aur FLVN01 us ke roles hain.

Exam trap · XK01 still exists — but BP is the answer

Older SAP used XK01 (create vendor), XK02 (change) and XK03 (display). In S/4HANA these transactions still open, but behind the screen they actually create a Business Partner — this is called CVI (Customer/Vendor Integration). If the exam asks “how is a vendor created in S/4HANA?”, the expected answer is transaction BP with BP roles. Purani screen khulti zaroor hai, magar andar se BP hi banta hai.

03 Vendor account groups & number ranges

Before SAP lets you create a vendor, it asks: what kind of vendor is this? That kind is the account group.

You met account groups for G/L accounts in Part 1 (OBD4): the group controlled the number range and which fields appear. Vendor masters work identically, with their own account groups, configured in OBD3.

Key term · Vendor account group

A vendor account group is the category a vendor belongs to — for example domestic vendors, foreign vendors, or one-time vendors. The group controls three things: (1) the number range the vendor number is drawn from, (2) which master-data fields are required, optional or hidden when the vendor is created, and (3) whether the vendor is a one-time vendor. Account group = vendor ki qism; number aur fields isi se tay hote hain.

Account group (typical)Used forNumber style
KREDNormal domestic vendors — your regular suppliersInternal number range, e.g. 100000–199999
ZFOR (example custom group)Foreign vendors — separate range and extra fields (e.g. foreign tax IDs)Own number range
CPDOne-time vendors — a supplier you will probably never use againOne shared master; name/address typed per invoice

Internal vs external numbering — same rule as G/L accounts

  • Internal assignment: SAP itself gives the next free number when you save (the normal case). You leave the vendor number blank in BP and SAP fills it.
  • External assignment: the user types the number — used when numbers must match an outside list. The account group decides which style applies.
Key term · One-time vendor

A one-time vendor is a shared, dummy master record (account group CPD) used for rare, one-off suppliers — say, a plumber called once for an emergency repair. Because the master is shared, SAP asks for the vendor's real name, address and bank details inside each invoice document itself. Aik dafa ke supplier ke liye alag master nahi banate — CPD wala mushtarika record use hota hai, asal tafseel invoice mein likhi jati hai.

Memory trick · Group pehle, number baad mein

Order yaad rakhein: pehle account group (qism), phir number range, phir vendor number. Exam jab poochta hai “vendor number kis cheez se tay hota hai?” — jawab hamesha account group hai.

04 The reconciliation account — AP's bridge to the G/L

The single most important AP idea in the whole C_TS4FI syllabus: how the vendor subledger and the General Ledger stay perfectly equal, automatically.

Here is the puzzle. The balance sheet must show one line: “Trade Payables — PKR 4,850,000”. But the AP subledger holds that total as hundreds of open bills across 60 vendors. If accountants had to post every bill twice — once in the subledger, once in the G/L — the two would drift apart within a week. SAP's answer is the reconciliation account.

Key term · Reconciliation account

A reconciliation account (often shortened to recon account) is a normal G/L account — for example 211000 Trade Payables — that is named inside the vendor's company code data. Whenever anything is posted to that vendor (invoice, payment, credit memo), SAP automatically posts the same amount to the recon account in the G/L, in the very same document. Subledger detail and G/L total can therefore never disagree. Recon account woh pul hai jo subledger ki har entry ko khud-ba-khud G/L tak pahuncha deta hai.

Three facts about the recon account — all exam favourites

  1. It is set in the vendor master (company code data, role FLVN00) — one field called “Recon. Account”. Change the field, and future postings go to the new account; old documents stay where they were posted.
  2. Direct posting is forbidden. Try to post to 211000 in FB50 and SAP refuses: a reconciliation account accepts postings only via its subledger. This protects the equality of the two ledgers.
  3. It is flagged in FS00. In the G/L master, the account's “reconciliation account for account type” is set to K (Vendors) — K stands for Kreditor, the German word for vendor. (D = Debitor = customers, for the AR recon account in Lesson 6.)
Analogy · Shop ki udhaar copy aur kul hisaab

Mohallay ki dukaan par har grahak ki alag copy (subledger) hoti hai, aur dukaan-daar ki diary mein sirf kul udhaar (G/L) likha hota hai. SAP mein copy mein entry hote hi diary ka kul khud badal jata hai — aur diary mein seedha entry karna mana hai, warna copy aur diary ka hisaab bigar jayega. Recon account wohi diary wala khata hai.

05 Step by step — BP: create a vendor

Now do it: create the vendor that the rest of this lesson will use — Punjab Textile Suppliers, a cloth mill that sells fabric to company code PK01.

  1. Type BP and press Enter. Choose Create Organisation (a company vendor; “Person” is for an individual).
  2. Select the BP grouping — the BP-side grouping works like an account group: it decides the number range. Our example grouping gives the BP/vendor number automatically (internal numbering), e.g. 100045.
  3. Fill the general data: name “Punjab Textile Suppliers”, address in Faisalabad, country PK, and the tax number (NTN) on the Identification tab.
  4. Add the vendor's bank details (bank key / IBAN) on the Payment Transactions tab — payments later will be sent to this bank.
  5. Now add the accounting hat: in the role dropdown choose FLVN00 — Vendor (FI), and enter the company code PK01.
  6. In the company code data, fill the three fields that matter most: Recon. Account = 211000 (Trade Payables), Payment Terms (e.g. NT30 — net due in 30 days), and Payment Methods (e.g. T — bank transfer).
  7. If purchase orders will be used with this vendor, also add role FLVN01 — Vendor (Purchasing) and fill the purchasing organisation data (order currency PKR).
  8. Save. SAP confirms the Business Partner number. From this moment, FB60, MIRO and F110 can all use vendor 100045 in company code PK01.

Key term · Payment terms

Payment terms are the agreed rule for when an invoice must be paid and whether a cash discount is given for paying early — for example “2% discount if paid in 10 days, otherwise net due in 30”. SAP stores the terms code in the vendor master, copies it into every invoice, and from it calculates the due date and any discount automatically. Payment terms = kab pay karna hai, aur jaldi pay karne par kitni riayat milegi.

Exam trap · “Vendor created” but FB60 cannot find it

A BP with only general data is not yet a vendor for accounting. If role FLVN00 with the company code data (above all the recon account) is missing, FB60 will not accept the vendor for that company code. The fix is always: extend the BP with the FLVN00 role for that company code — the same “extend” idea as FS00 in Lesson 4.

06 Reading the vendor ledger — FBL1N & open items

The vendor equivalent of FBL3N from Lesson 3: one screen that answers “what do we owe, to whom, and is it due yet?”

In Lesson 3 you read G/L line items with FBL3N. Vendor line items have their own display: FBL1N — Vendor Line Item Display. Enter a vendor (or a range), a company code, and choose what to see: open items, cleared items, or all items.

Key term · Open item

An open item is a line on a vendor (or customer) account that is not yet settled — an invoice still waiting to be paid, or a payment not yet matched to an invoice. An open item becomes a cleared item when it is matched with its settlement (Section 13). Open items are the heartbeat of AP: the total of all open items on a vendor = exactly what you owe that vendor today. Open item = woh bill jo abhi pay nahi hua; cleared = hisaab barabar ho gaya.

How to read an FBL1N line

  • Status symbol: red/open = still unpaid; green/cleared = settled, with a clearing document number and clearing date attached.
  • Due date: calculated from the baseline date plus the payment terms. Overdue open items are the ones payment runs and managers chase first.
  • Sign: vendor invoices appear as credits (negative) — from the company's view, money owed. Payments appear as debits, reducing what is owed.
  • Double-click any line to drill into the full document — the same FB03-style display you learned in Lessons 3 and 4.
Key term · Baseline date

The baseline date is the starting date from which SAP counts the payment terms to find the due date — usually the invoice date. Terms NT30 + baseline 12.11.2026 → due 12.12.2026. Baseline date woh din hai jahan se gin-ti shuru hoti hai ke bill kab due hoga.

07 Step by step — FB60: enter a vendor invoice

The workhorse of AP: booking a supplier's bill when there is no purchase order behind it — services, rent, utilities, one-off purchases.

FB60 is the Enjoy screen for vendor invoices — the same friendly one-screen style as FB50, but built for vendors. It creates document type KR (vendor invoice) and uses two posting keys behind the scenes: 31 (credit vendor) for the vendor line and 40 (debit G/L) for the expense or asset lines.

  1. Type FB60 and press Enter. Confirm the company code (PK01).
  2. Enter the Vendor number (100045). Press Enter — SAP pulls the vendor's name, payment terms and recon account from the master automatically.
  3. Fill the header: Invoice date (the date on the supplier's bill), Posting date, Reference = the supplier's own invoice number (e.g. INV-2211 — your bridge back to the paper bill), and the Amount of the bill (gross, including any tax).
  4. Check the payment terms and baseline date SAP proposes from the vendor master; the due date is calculated for you.
  5. In the item table, enter the other side: G/L account (e.g. 610200 Cloth Purchases, or a stock account), the amount, a text, and the cost centre if the field status group demands one.
  6. If the bill carries sales tax / VAT, use the Tax tab: tick “Calculate tax” or enter the tax code — SAP posts the input tax to the tax account itself (input tax is covered in depth in Lesson 6; here just know the tab exists).
  7. Watch the balance light: the vendor credit must equal the total of your debit lines. Green = zero difference.
  8. Simulate, check the document — you should see the vendor line with posting key 31 and the recon account shown in the vendor line's details — then Post. SAP replies: “Document 1900000118 was posted in company code PK01.”

Key term · Document type KR

KR is the document type for vendor invoices entered in FI (FB60 / F-43). Its number range gives AP invoices their own document numbers (our example: 1900000118). Remember the family: KR vendor invoice, KG vendor credit memo, KZ vendor payment — all start with K for Kreditor. K wala har document vendor se mutaliq hai.

08 MIRO — invoice verification against a purchase order

When goods were ordered formally, the invoice is not typed freely — it is checked against the order and the delivery. That checking has its own transaction: MIRO.

Big purchases in SAP run through MM (Materials Management): first a purchase order (PO) is sent to the vendor, then the goods arrive and a goods receipt is recorded, and only then does the vendor's invoice arrive. Finance must not pay for what was never ordered or never received — so the invoice is entered in MIRO, Logistics Invoice Verification, where SAP compares all three documents.

Key term · Purchase order (PO)

A purchase order is the company's formal order to a vendor: what, how much, at what price, by when. It is a purchasing (MM) document, created with ME21N. It does not post anything in accounting — it is a promise, not an entry. PO = farmayish (order); hisaab-kitab ki entry nahi hoti, sirf commitment hota hai.

Key term · Three-way match

The three-way match is SAP's automatic check that three documents agree before an invoice is accepted: (1) the purchase order — what we ordered and at what price; (2) the goods receipt — what actually arrived; (3) the vendor invoice — what the vendor is charging. Quantity or price differences beyond a small tolerance make SAP block the invoice for payment until a human resolves them. Teen kagaz milao — order, raseed (delivery), bill. Teeno barabar hon to hi payment banta hai.

Step by step — MIRO in outline

  1. Type MIRO. Choose transaction type Invoice.
  2. Enter the invoice date, posting date, the gross amount from the bill, and the vendor's invoice number as Reference.
  3. On the PO Reference tab, enter the purchase order number. SAP pulls in the PO items with quantities and prices from the order and the goods receipts.
  4. Compare: SAP shows ordered vs delivered vs invoiced quantities and amounts. Small differences within tolerance are accepted (and posted as small price differences); big ones set a payment block.
  5. Simulate, then Post. MIRO creates an MM invoice document and an FI document (type RE) in one step: the vendor is credited, and the debit side clears the GR/IR account (see below) instead of an expense account.
Key term · GR/IR account

GR/IR (Goods Receipt / Invoice Receipt) is a clearing account used in the PO process. When goods arrive (before the bill), SAP credits GR/IR: “goods received, bill not yet received”. When the invoice is verified in MIRO, SAP debits GR/IR and credits the vendor — the temporary balance washes out. If GR/IR still has a balance, either goods came without a bill, or a bill came without goods — both need follow-up. GR/IR aik darmiyani khata hai — maal pehle aye ya bill pehle, farq yahin park hota hai.

Exam trap · FB60 vs MIRO

The exam loves this pair. FB60 = invoice WITHOUT a purchase order reference (direct FI entry; you type the G/L lines yourself). MIRO = invoice WITH a purchase order (logistics invoice verification; SAP proposes the lines from the PO and performs the three-way match). If a question mentions a PO, goods receipt or a payment block from a price variance — the answer is MIRO, never FB60.

09 F-43 & FB65 — the classic invoice screen and the credit memo

Two more AP entry screens: FB60's classic twin, and the screen that undoes part of an invoice when goods come back.

F-43 — the classic vendor invoice

F-43 does exactly what FB60 does — posts a KR vendor invoice — but in the classic style you met in F-02: first a header screen, then one screen per line item, and you type the posting keys yourself: 31 for the vendor credit line, then 40 for each G/L debit line. Same document, same result — only the typing experience differs. Consultants keep F-43 for special cases (for example, when a specific posting key or a one-time vendor's address data must be entered line by line).

FB60 (Enjoy)F-43 (Classic)

Posting keysChosen via D/C logic — SAP fills 31 / 40Typed by you: 31 vendor credit, 40 G/L debit
Document typeKRKR
ResultIdentical vendor invoice document and open item

FB65 — the vendor credit memo

Sometimes the vendor owes you a reduction: damaged cloth is returned, the bill had a wrong rate, a rebate is agreed. The vendor sends a credit memo — a document that reduces what you owe. Enter it in FB65 (classic twin: F-41), document type KG.

Key term · Credit memo

A credit memo (vendor side) is the mirror image of an invoice: it debits the vendor (posting key 21) and credits the expense/asset account, reducing the vendor's open balance. Later, the credit memo is cleared against invoices (F-44) so the net amount is what actually gets paid. Credit memo = bill mein kami ka kagaz — vendor ka udhaar kam ho jata hai.

  1. Type FB65, enter the vendor, dates, reference (the vendor's credit memo number) and the amount.
  2. Enter the G/L line as a credit — usually the same expense or stock account the original invoice debited.
  3. Simulate and post. The vendor now has a debit open item (negative payable) sitting next to the credit invoices.
  4. When paying, clear the credit memo together with an invoice (F-44 or inside the payment) so only the net is paid.
Memory trick · KR khareed, KG kami

KR = invoice = vendor ka haq barhta hai (credit). KG = credit memo = vendor ka haq ghat-ta hai (debit). Aur dono ka classic/Enjoy jora yaad rakhein: FB60 ↔ F-43 (invoice), FB65 ↔ F-41 (credit memo) — Enjoy hamesha FB se, classic hamesha F- se shuru hota hai.

10 Worked example — a purchase, from invoice to open item

One complete story with real numbers — the same rhythm as Lesson 4's rent example, now through the AP subledger.

The business events: On 12 November 2026, Punjab Textile Suppliers (vendor 100045) delivers cloth to Ibrahim Apparel & Trading (PK01) with bill INV-2211 for PKR 200,000, terms NT30. The cloth was bought without a purchase order, so the invoice is entered in FB60. A week later, 20,000 worth of cloth is found damaged and returned; the vendor issues credit memo CM-087 for PKR 20,000, entered in FB65.

AP documents posted · ILLUSTRATION
12.11.2026 · FB60 · Type KR · Doc 1900000118 Dr 610200 Cloth Purchases ......... 200,000 (key 40) Cr Vendor 100045 ................... 200,000 (key 31 → recon 211000) 19.11.2026 · FB65 · Type KG · Doc 1900000131 Dr Vendor 100045 ................... 20,000 (key 21) Cr 610200 Cloth Purchases ......... 20,000 (key 50) ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── Vendor 100045 open items in FBL1N: 200,000 Cr (open) · 20,000 Dr (open) Net payable: 180,000 Due date of the invoice: 12.12.2026 (NT30 from 12.11)

Follow the chain inside SAP

  1. Invoice posted (FB60): vendor line key 31 credits vendor 100045; SAP simultaneously credits recon account 211000 in the G/L — one document, two ledgers (Section 4). The bill is now an open item with due date 12.12.2026.
  2. Credit memo posted (FB65): vendor line key 21 debits the vendor; recon account is debited automatically. A second, opposite open item appears.
  3. Balances: Cloth Purchases shows a net debit of 180,000; Trade Payables (211000) shows a net credit of 180,000; FBL1N for the vendor shows both open items totalling the same 180,000 credit. Three views, one truth — always.
  4. Still open: nothing is cleared yet. Clearing happens when the net 180,000 is paid and matched — Sections 11–13.
Analogy · Do kagaz, aik asal dena

Bill 200,000 ka aya, wapsi ka parcha 20,000 ka — dukaan-daar ki copy mein dono likhe hain, magar asal dena 180,000 hai. SAP bhi dono open items alag-alag dikhata hai; payment ke waqt dono ko mila (clear) karke asal raqam di jati hai. Credit memo invoice ko delete nahi karta — us ke saath khara rehta hai.

11 Paying one vendor by hand — F-53 outgoing payment

One vendor, one payment, done manually — F-53 posts the payment and clears the invoice in a single sitting.

When a payment is urgent or a vendor must be paid outside the normal payment cycle, accountants pay manually with F-53 — Post Outgoing Payment. It creates document type KZ (vendor payment). “Outgoing” simply means money goes out of our bank to the vendor.

  1. Type F-53. Fill the header: document date, posting date, company code PK01, type KZ.
  2. In the Bank data area, enter the paying bank G/L account (e.g. 110100 Bank – Main) and the amount being paid — for our worked example, the net 180,000.
  3. In Open item selection, enter the vendor 100045. SAP lists the vendor's open items: invoice 200,000 Cr and credit memo 20,000 Dr.
  4. Select (activate) both items so their net (180,000 Cr) exactly equals the payment amount. The “not assigned” difference must be zero — SAP will not post a payment with an unexplained difference (small differences within the vendor's tolerance group can be written off to a difference account, but zero is the goal).
  5. Simulate: you should see Bank credited 180,000 (key 50) and the vendor debited (key 21) against the cleared items. Post.
  6. Result: a KZ payment document is created, and both open items become cleared — stamped with this payment document as their clearing document and today's date as the clearing date.

Key term · Outgoing payment

An outgoing payment is any payment the company makes to a vendor — Dr vendor, Cr bank. Its mirror in Lesson 6 is the incoming payment from a customer. Document type for vendor payments: KZ. Outgoing payment = hamare bank se vendor ko gaya paisa.

12 F110 — the automatic payment program

Paying one vendor by hand is fine. Paying sixty vendors every week is not — that is what the payment program is for.

F110 is the Automatic Payment Program: you tell SAP which company codes, which vendors, up to which due date and with which payment methods to pay — and SAP selects all due open items, takes cash discounts where offered, posts every payment document, and produces the bank file / cheques. One run can pay hundreds of vendors.

The five steps, in plain words

  1. Parameters: enter the run date and an identification (e.g. PKW01), the posting date, company code(s), payment method(s) (e.g. T = transfer, C = cheque), the next posting date (so SAP also catches items becoming due just after this run), and the vendor range.
  2. Proposal run: SAP scans the open items and builds a proposal — who would be paid what. Nothing is posted yet. Items it cannot pay (blocked, missing bank details, no valid payment method) land on the exception list — always read it.
  3. Edit proposal (optional): block an item you dispute, change a payment method or bank, split a payment. Maker-checker control lives here.
  4. Payment run: the real step. SAP posts one KZ payment document per vendor (or per group of items), clears the paid open items, and books any cash discount taken to a discount-received account.
  5. Payment media: SAP produces the output the bank needs — a payment file for online banking, or printed cheques / remittance advices (payment summaries sent to vendors).
Key term · Payment method & FBZP

A payment method is a one-letter code for how money moves — e.g. T transfer, C cheque. Which methods exist, which house bank pays them, and from which bank account, is configured once in FBZP (the payment program configuration) — the exam expects you to know FBZP as F110's setup transaction. F110 chalata hai payment; FBZP us ki setting hai — bank, tareeqa, sab wahan tay hota hai.

Exam trap · Proposal ≠ payment

Running the proposal creates no documents and clears nothing — balances do not move. Only the payment run posts. Exam questions that say “the proposal was run; what is the vendor's balance?” — answer: unchanged. And a vendor whose master lacks bank details or an allowed payment method appears in the exception list, unpaid.

13 Clearing vendor open items — F-44

Sometimes two open items cancel each other and no money moves at all. Matching them up is called clearing — and it has its own transaction.

You saw clearing inside F-53: the payment was matched with the invoice and credit memo, and all three became “cleared”. But clearing is also needed without any payment — for example, an invoice and a credit memo of the same amount, or an invoice and a down payment (Section 15). That manual matching is F-44 — Clear Vendor.

Key term · Clearing

Clearing means marking two or more open items on an account as settled against each other. SAP stamps each item with the clearing document number and clearing date, and the items move from “open” to “cleared” in FBL1N. Cleared items are never deleted — the history stays visible forever. A clearing must balance to zero: the selected debits must equal the selected credits. Clearing = do open items ko aik dusre ke khilaf barabar karke band kar dena.

  1. Type F-44. Enter the vendor and company code; optionally limit by date or document numbers.
  2. SAP lists the vendor's open items. Activate the items that cancel each other — e.g. invoice 50,000 Cr and credit memo 50,000 Dr.
  3. Check the total at the bottom: selected debits = selected credits, difference zero.
  4. Save. SAP posts a small clearing document (type AB) whose only job is to carry the clearing stamp, and both items become cleared.

Clearing vs resetting — one warning

If items were cleared by mistake, clearing can be undone with FBRA — Reset Cleared Items. FBRA offers two choices the exam distinguishes carefully:

  • Reset only: the clearing stamp is removed; the items become open again. The payment document itself stays posted.
  • Reset and reverse: the clearing is removed and the clearing/payment document is reversed (an FB08-style mirror document is created).
Exam trap · You cannot reverse a cleared document directly

Trying FB08 on a document whose items are cleared fails — SAP protects the clearing link. The correct order is: FBRA first (reset the clearing, items open again), then FB08 if the document itself must be reversed. This two-step order appears in almost every C_TS4FI paper.

14 Partial payment vs residual payment

The vendor is owed 200,000 but today you pay only 150,000. SAP gives you two different ways to record that — and the exam tests the difference hard.

In F-53 (or in F110), after selecting the open invoice, you enter the amount actually paid on the payment amount side. SAP then asks, in effect: “the invoice is not fully covered — how should the remaining 50,000 live on?” There are two answers:

Partial payment Invoice stays open

The payment is posted as a separate new open item on the vendor account, linked to the invoice as a partial payment. The original invoice stays open at its full 200,000. FBL1N afterwards shows: invoice 200,000 Cr (open) + partial payment 150,000 Dr (open) = net open 50,000.

Invoice apni asal raqam par khuli rehti hai; payment alag open item ban jata hai.

Residual payment Invoice is cleared

The original invoice is cleared (closed) by the payment, and SAP creates a new open item for just the leftover difference — the “residual item” of 50,000 Cr, with its own document number and (usually) the original due date. FBL1N afterwards shows only: residual item 50,000 Cr (open).

Invoice band ho jata hai; sirf baqaya raqam ka naya open item reh jata hai.

15 Down payments — F-47 request & F-48 payment

Some vendors want money before they deliver — an advance. SAP handles advances with a special two-step mechanism you must not confuse with a normal payment.

Suppose a mill demands 30% advance — PKR 60,000 — before weaving a special order. If you simply posted a normal F-53 payment, SAP would try to clear it against open invoices — but there is no invoice yet; the goods have not even been made. An advance is not a settlement of a bill; it is a different kind of receivable-from-vendor, and the balance sheet must show it separately (as “advance to suppliers”, an asset-side item, not netted inside trade payables). SAP solves this with special G/L transactions.

Key term · Special G/L indicator

A special G/L indicator is a one-letter flag on a vendor/customer line that diverts the posting away from the normal reconciliation account to an alternative reconciliation account. For vendor down payments the indicator is A: the line posts not to 211000 Trade Payables but to a separate account such as 212000 Advances to Vendors. Same vendor, different G/L home — because an advance is legally a different thing from a payable. Special G/L indicator woh switch hai jo entry ko aam recon account ke bajaye khaas (alternative) account mein bhej deta hai.

Step 1 — F-47: the down payment request

  1. Type F-47. Enter the vendor, the requested amount (60,000), the due date by which the advance should be paid, and special G/L indicator A.
  2. Post. The request becomes a noted item on the vendor account.
Key term · Noted item

A noted item is a memo entry on a vendor/customer account: it is visible as an open item and the payment program can pick it up — but it posts only one line, with no offsetting entry, and changes no G/L balances. It is a reminder with a due date, not a real posting. The down payment request (F-47) is the classic noted item. Noted item = sirf yaad-dihani wala parcha; balance par koi asar nahi.

Step 2 — F-48: the down payment itself

  1. Type F-48 — Post Vendor Down Payment. Enter the bank account and the amount 60,000, and the vendor with special G/L indicator A. (If an F-47 request exists, F110 can also pay it automatically, exactly like an invoice.)
  2. Post: Dr Vendor 100045 (indicator A) 60,000 → lands on alternative recon account 212000 Advances to Vendors; Cr Bank 60,000. A real document, real balances — but kept apart from trade payables.
  3. Later, the goods arrive and the real invoice (say 200,000) is posted normally to 211000. Now the advance must be cleared against the invoice (transfer/clearing of the down payment, F-54): the 60,000 moves from Advances to Vendors against the payable, and only the remaining 140,000 is paid in cash.
Down payment flow · ILLUSTRATION
Step 1 F-47 Request 60,000 noted item — no balances change Step 2 F-48 Down payment Dr Vendor (A) 60,000 → 212000 Advances · Cr Bank 60,000 Step 3 FB60 Invoice 200,000 Dr Purchases 200,000 · Cr Vendor → 211000 Payables Step 4 F-54 Clear down payment advance moved against the invoice Step 5 F-53 / F110 Pay balance only 140,000 cash leaves the bank
Exam trap · Request vs payment vs normal payment

Three different things: F-47 request = noted item, no balance effect. F-48 down payment = real posting, but to the alternative recon account via indicator A. F-53 normal payment = settles existing invoices through the normal recon account. If a question says “advance paid before delivery, shown separately in the balance sheet” — it is F-48 with special G/L indicator A, every time.

16 Exam traps

The AP questions C_TS4FI asks again and again — each one is a trap for the half-prepared. Read every one.

Trap 1 · Posting directly to the reconciliation account

“Can a user post a manual entry to the AP reconciliation account in FB50?” — Never. Recon accounts accept postings only through the subledger (vendor/customer lines). Any option that posts directly to 211000 is wrong on sight.

Trap 2 · Vendor creation in S/4HANA

Vendors are created as Business Partners in BP with roles (FLVN00 for FI, FLVN01 for purchasing). XK01 merely calls the same BP machinery through CVI. “XK01 is the primary way in S/4HANA” is the trap answer.

Trap 3 · FB60 vs MIRO

PO mentioned, goods receipt mentioned, price variance block mentioned → MIRO. No PO, direct expense/service bill → FB60 (or F-43). Mixing these two is the most common AP mistake in the exam hall.

Trap 4 · Proposal run posts nothing

In F110, the proposal is only a draft list — no documents, no clearing, no balance change. Only the payment run posts KZ documents. And items that fail (blocked, missing bank data, no payment method) appear in the exception list, not in the payments.

Trap 5 · Partial vs residual

“After the payment, the original invoice remains open at its full amount” = partial payment. “The invoice is cleared and a new open item for the difference is created” = residual payment. The examiners swap these two definitions on purpose — anchor on the fate of the original invoice.

Trap 6 · Down payment request has no balance effect

F-47 creates a noted item: single-sided, no G/L posting. F-48 is the real payment, and it posts via special G/L indicator A to an alternative reconciliation account — never to the normal trade payables account.

Trap 7 · Reversing a cleared document

FB08 refuses a document with cleared items. Correct sequence: FBRA (reset clearing) → then FB08 (reverse). “Reset only” reopens the items; “reset and reverse” also reverses the clearing document.

Trap 8 · What the account group controls

The vendor account group (OBD3) controls the number range, the field status of the master record, and the one-time-vendor flag. It does not hold the recon account — that lives in the vendor's company code data. Questions swap these two locations deliberately.

17 A–Z glossary

Every technical term from this lesson, in one place, in simple words.

Account group (vendor)
The vendor's category (OBD3) controlling its number range, which master fields are required/optional/hidden, and the one-time-vendor flag.
Accounts Payable (AP)
The subledger that tracks, vendor by vendor and bill by bill, everything the company owes its suppliers.
Baseline date
The date from which payment terms are counted to calculate an item's due date — usually the invoice date.
BP (Business Partner)
The central S/4HANA master record for a person or organisation; transaction BP. Roles turn it into a vendor or customer.
Clearing
Matching open items against each other (invoice with payment / credit memo / down payment) so they become “cleared”. Done inside F-53 / F110 or manually in F-44.
Credit memo (FB65)
A vendor document that reduces what you owe (damaged goods, price correction). Type KG; debits the vendor (key 21). Classic twin: F-41.
CVI
Customer/Vendor Integration — the S/4HANA mechanism by which old transactions like XK01 create Business Partners behind the scenes.
Down payment
An advance paid to a vendor before delivery. Requested in F-47 (noted item), paid in F-48 with special G/L indicator A to an alternative recon account, cleared against the final invoice (F-54).
F-43
Classic vendor invoice transaction — same KR document as FB60, but posting keys (31/40) are typed line by line.
F-44
Clear vendor open items manually, without a payment — e.g. invoice against an equal credit memo. Produces an AB clearing document.
F-47 / F-48
F-47 = down payment request (noted item, no balance effect). F-48 = post the vendor down payment (real posting, indicator A).
F-53
Post outgoing payment manually: one vendor paid from a bank account, selected open items cleared in the same step. Type KZ.
F110
The Automatic Payment Program: parameters → proposal → edit → payment run → payment media. Configuration is done in FBZP.
FB60
Enjoy transaction to enter a vendor invoice without a purchase order. Type KR; keys 31 (Cr vendor) and 40 (Dr G/L) are filled automatically.
FB65
Enjoy transaction to enter a vendor credit memo. Type KG.
FBRA
Reset cleared items: removes the clearing stamp (items open again), optionally also reversing the clearing document. Required before FB08 can reverse a cleared document.
FBL1N
Vendor line item display — open, cleared or all items for one or more vendors; the AP equivalent of FBL3N.
FLVN00 / FLVN01
BP roles for a vendor: FLVN00 = Vendor (FI), company code data incl. recon account; FLVN01 = Vendor (Purchasing), purchasing organisation data.
GR/IR account
Goods Receipt / Invoice Receipt clearing account in the PO process: credited at goods receipt, debited at invoice verification (MIRO).
MIRO
Logistics Invoice Verification — entering a vendor invoice against a purchase order, with the three-way match (PO ↔ goods receipt ↔ invoice). FI document type RE.
Noted item
A one-sided memo item on a vendor/customer account (e.g. a down payment request): visible and payable via F110, but with no G/L balance effect.
One-time vendor
A shared dummy master (account group CPD) for rare suppliers; the real name, address and bank details are entered in each document itself.
Open item
A subledger line not yet settled — an unpaid invoice or unmatched payment. Cleared items carry a clearing document number and date.
Outgoing payment
A payment from the company to a vendor (Dr vendor, Cr bank). Type KZ.
Partial payment
Paying less than the invoice while the invoice stays open at its full amount; the payment becomes its own open item.
Payment terms
The agreed due-date and cash-discount rule (e.g. NT30) stored in the vendor master and copied into each invoice.
Purchase order (PO)
The formal MM order to a vendor (ME21N). A commitment only — no accounting posting until goods receipt / invoice.
Reconciliation account
The G/L account named in the vendor's company code data; every vendor posting updates it automatically in the same document. Direct posting to it is forbidden. Flagged in FS00 with recon type K (vendors).
Residual payment
Paying less than the invoice where the invoice is cleared and a new open item (the residual item) is created for the leftover difference.
Special G/L indicator
A one-letter flag (e.g. A for down payments) that posts a subledger line to an alternative reconciliation account instead of the normal one.
Three-way match
MIRO's check that purchase order, goods receipt and vendor invoice agree in quantity and price before the invoice is accepted for payment.
Vendor
SAP's word for a supplier — a person or company the business buys from and owes money to.

18 Summary — the whole lesson on one screen

If you remember one screen before the exam, make it this one.

  • Accounts Payable is the vendor subledger under one G/L total. A vendor in S/4HANA is a Business Partner (BP) with roles FLVN00 (FI / company code data) and FLVN01 (purchasing).
  • The vendor account group (OBD3) controls number range, master-data field status and the one-time-vendor flag (CPD = one-time).
  • The reconciliation account (set in the vendor's company code data; flagged “K” in FS00) is updated automatically by every vendor posting in the same document. Direct posting to it is impossible.
  • FBL1N shows vendor line items. An open item is unsettled; due dates come from baseline date + payment terms.
  • FB60 = vendor invoice without PO (type KR, keys 31/40). MIRO = invoice against a PO with the three-way match (PO ↔ GR ↔ invoice) and the GR/IR account; blocked if variances exceed tolerance. F-43 is FB60's classic twin; FB65 posts a credit memo (type KG).
  • F-53 pays one vendor manually (type KZ) and clears in the same step. F110 pays many vendors: parameters → proposal (posts nothing) → edit → payment run (posts KZ documents) → payment media. Setup lives in FBZP; failures land on the exception list.
  • F-44 clears open items against each other without money moving. Wrong clearing → FBRA reset (before any FB08 reversal of a cleared document).
  • Partial payment: invoice stays open in full; payment is a separate open item. Residual payment: invoice is cleared; a new residual open item carries the difference.
  • Down payments: F-47 request = noted item, no balance effect. F-48 payment = real posting with special G/L indicator A to an alternative recon account (advances), later cleared against the final invoice (F-54) so only the balance is paid in cash.
One-line memory chain

BP vendor banata hai · Recon account pul hai · FB60 bill, MIRO order wala bill · FB65 kami ka parcha · F-53 haath se, F110 machine se payment · F-44 milan (clearing) · Advance F-47/F-48, indicator A. — BP creates the vendor, the recon account is the bridge, FB60 bills without a PO and MIRO with one, FB65 reduces, F-53 pays by hand and F110 by machine, F-44 matches items, and advances run through F-47/F-48 with indicator A.

01 Where the course stands — from owing money to being owed money

Lesson 5 was about money going out. This lesson is about money coming in — and the tax that rides on both.

In Part 2 · Lesson 5 you ran the full vendor cycle in Accounts Payable: master, invoice, payment, clearing. Now turn the whole picture around. When Ibrahim Apparel & Trading sells winter jackets to a retailer in Lahore on 30 days' credit, the company needs the same kind of detailed side-ledger — but this time for the people who owe us money. That side-ledger is Accounts Receivable, shortened to AR.

Key term · Accounts Receivable (AR)

Accounts Receivable is the subledger (detailed side-book) for customers — everyone who has bought from you on credit and still has to pay. The G/L shows one total, “Trade Receivables”; the AR subledger shows the detail — customer by customer, invoice by invoice, due date by due date. Accounts Receivable = customers ka tafseeli register — kis ne kitna dena hai, kab dena hai.

Key term · Customer

In SAP language, a buyer is called a customer (in German-based SAP terms, a Debitor — which is why customer things so often carry the letter D). One customer = one master record + one account in the AR subledger. Customer = khareedar, jis ne udhaar par maal liya aur paisay dene hain.

The good news: the AR cycle is the AP cycle in a mirror. If you understood Lesson 5, you already know the shape of this lesson:

Two extra AR tools sit on top of this cycle, and both are exam favourites. Dunning is SAP's polite-but-firm reminder machine for customers who pay late. Credit management is the guard at the gate: it stops you from selling even more to a customer who has already reached their safe limit. And in the second half of the lesson you will meet tax — because almost every invoice in this lesson, and almost every invoice in Lesson 5, quietly carries a tax line that SAP calculates for you.

Memory trick · AP ulta = AR

Vendor ko dena hai, customer se lena hai. AP mein invoice vendor ko credit karta hai (hamari liability barhti hai); AR mein invoice customer ko debit karta hai (hamara asset barhta hai). Baaki saara khel — master, open item, payment, clearing — bilkul wohi hai, bas direction ulti hai.

02 The customer master & the Business Partner (BP)

Just like vendors, customers in S/4HANA are Business Partners — the same central record, wearing a different hat.

You already know this idea from Lesson 5: in S/4HANA there is no separate old-style customer master. A customer is created as a Business Partner in transaction BP, and roles are added to say what that partner does. For a customer, the roles are the mirror of the vendor roles:

BP roleWhat it addsAP twin (Lesson 5)
FLCU00 — Customer (FI)The company code data: reconciliation account, payment terms, dunning procedure, tolerance groupFLVN00 — Vendor (FI)
FLCU01 — Customer (Sales)The sales area data: sales organisation, distribution channel, division — used by the Sales (SD) side for orders and billingFLVN01 — Vendor (Purchasing)
Key term · Role FLCU00 / FLCU01

FLCU00 is the accounting “hat” of a customer — without it, FI cannot post an invoice to that customer. FLCU01 is the sales “hat” — without it, the sales team cannot create a sales order for that customer. Remember the pattern: VN = vendor, CU = customer, 00 = FI view, 01 = logistics view. FLCU00 = customer ka FI role (hisaab-kitab), FLCU01 = customer ka sales role.

The customer master has three layers

Exactly like the vendor master, the customer master is built in layers — each maintained by a different team:

Layer (segment)What it holdsWho uses it
General data (client level — valid everywhere)Name, address, phone, email, tax numbers (e.g. the customer's NTN / sales tax registration)Everyone — entered once for the whole system
Company code data (FI view — per company code)Reconciliation account, payment terms, dunning procedure, credit-related fields, tolerance groupFinance (FI) — invoices FB70, payments F-28, dunning F150
Sales area data (per sales area)Sales terms, shipping and billing settings for ordersSales (SD) — sales orders and billing documents
Key term · Sales area

A sales area is the combination of sales organisation + distribution channel + division — in plain words: which sales unit is selling, through which channel (wholesale, retail, online…), and which product family. Customer sales data is maintained per sales area, just as FI data is maintained per company code. Sales area = kaunsi sales team, kis channel se, kaunsi product line bech rahi hai.

Analogy · Aik dukaan, do register

Sochain ek mashhoor kapray ki dukaan hai jo hum se udhaar par jackets leti hai. Wahi dukaan kabhi kabhi hum ko purana stock wapas bhi bech deti hai. SAP mein is ke do alag record nahi bante — aik hi Business Partner hota hai, jis par customer role (FLCU00) bhi hai aur vendor role (FLVN00) bhi. Aik shakhs, do kirdar — bilkul Lesson 5 wali CNIC wali misaal.

Exam trap · XD01 still exists — but BP is the answer

Older SAP used XD01 (create customer), XD02 (change) and XD03 (display). In S/4HANA they still open, but behind the screen they create a Business Partner through CVI (Customer/Vendor Integration) — the same mechanism you met for vendors. If the exam asks how a customer is created in S/4HANA, the expected answer is transaction BP with BP roles. Purani screen khulti zaroor hai, magar andar se BP hi banta hai.

03 Customer account groups & number ranges

Before SAP lets you create a customer, it asks the same question it asked for vendors: what kind of customer is this?

Customer masters use account groups exactly like vendor masters do — only the configuration transaction and the standard group names differ. Customer account groups are configured in OBD2 (vendors were OBD3).

Key term · Customer account group

A customer account group is the category a customer belongs to — for example sold-to parties, ship-to parties, or one-time customers. The group controls three things: (1) the number range the customer number is drawn from, (2) which master-data fields are required, optional or hidden when the customer is created, and (3) whether the customer is a one-time customer. Account group = customer ki qism; number aur fields isi se tay hote hain.

Account group (typical SAP standard)Used forNumber style
0001 — Sold-to partyThe normal customer who orders, receives the invoice and pays — your regular buyersInternal number range, e.g. 100000–199999
0002 — Ship-to partyAn address goods are delivered to (may be different from who pays)Own number range
CPD / CPDA — One-time customerA walk-in buyer you will probably never trade with againOne shared master; name/address typed per invoice
Key term · One-time customer

A one-time customer uses one shared master record for many rare buyers. Because the master holds no real name or address, SAP asks for the customer's name, address and bank details inside each invoice instead. One-time customer = aik shared record; asal naam-pata har invoice mein alag se likhna parta hai.

Key term · Partner functions

In the sales world, one business deal can involve several partners with different jobs: the sold-to party (places the order), the ship-to party (receives the goods), the bill-to party (receives the invoice) and the payer (actually pays). These jobs are called partner functions. In FI you mostly meet the payer — the one whose account the invoice and payment hit. Partner functions = aik deal mein har partner ka apna kaam: order dene wala, maal lene wala, bill lene wala, paisay dene wala.

Exam trap · OBD2 vs OBD3 — one letter apart

Customer account groups: OBD2. Vendor account groups: OBD3. G/L account groups (Part 1): OBD4. The exam loves this family. Memory line: “2 = Customer (Debitor), 3 = Vendor (Kreditor), 4 = G/L.” OBD2 customer, OBD3 vendor, OBD4 G/L — number ke saath yaad kar lein.

04 The reconciliation account — AR's bridge to the G/L

The single most important field in the customer's company code data — and the exam's favourite AR question.

You know this mechanism from AP; it works identically here, in the opposite direction. The AR subledger holds the detail for every customer, but the balance sheet cannot list hundreds of customers — it needs one G/L account: “Trade Receivables”. The bridge between the two is the reconciliation account (short: recon account).

Key term · Reconciliation account (AR)

The reconciliation account is the G/L account entered in the customer's company code data (role FLCU00). Every time anything is posted to that customer — invoice, credit memo, payment, clearing — SAP updates this G/L account automatically, in the same document. That is why the AR subledger total and the G/L balance can never drift apart. Recon account woh pul hai jo customer ke hisaab ko G/L se har posting mein khud-ba-khud jor deta hai.

Three facts the exam rotates around

  • Where it lives: in the customer's company code data — so the same customer can point to a different recon account in a different company code.
  • How the G/L account is marked: in FS00, a customer recon account carries reconciliation account type D (D = Debitor = customer). Vendor recon accounts carry type K (Kreditor). Exam favourite
  • Direct posting is impossible: you can never type the recon account into FB50/FB70 as a normal line. SAP protects it so the subledger and G/L can never disagree. If you try, SAP stops you with an error.
Memory trick · D lena, K dena

D = Debitor = customer = hum ne lena hai. K = Kreditor = vendor = hum ne dena hai. D-lena, K-dena — recon account type kabhi confuse nahi hoga.

05 Step by step — BP: create a customer

Now the practical part: creating a customer from an empty screen to a postable master record.

Scenario: a Faisalabad retailer, City Style Garments, will buy winter stock from us on credit. Before the first FB70 invoice can exist, the customer master must exist. Here is the full path:

  1. Open transaction BP and choose Create Organisation (a company) — or Create Person for an individual.
  2. Select the BP grouping — the grouping decides the number range the new BP number comes from, just like the account group did. SAP assigns the number (internal numbering) or you type it (external numbering), as the grouping allows.
  3. Fill the general data: name “City Style Garments”, address, city, country, language, and tax numbers on the Identification tab.
  4. Add the role FLCU00 — Customer (FI). A new view opens: enter the company code, then the FI fields — above all the reconciliation account (e.g. Trade Receivables), plus payment terms (e.g. 30 days net) and the dunning procedure (needed later in Section 13).
  5. If the sales team will also sell to this customer through SAP sales orders, add role FLCU01 — Customer (Sales) and fill the sales area data.
  6. Save. SAP confirms the Business Partner number — and because of CVI, a linked customer number exists with the same or a mapped number.

Key term · BP grouping

The grouping chosen at BP creation controls the BP's number range — it is the BP-level cousin of the account group. Account groups still exist on the FI side (they drive field status for the customer data created through CVI), so both settings work together. Grouping = BP ka number kis range se aayega; account group = fields kaise behave karenge.

Exam trap · “Created” is not “postable”

A BP with only general data cannot receive an FI invoice. The customer becomes postable in a company code only when role FLCU00 is extended to that company code and a reconciliation account is entered. Forgetting the recon account is the most common beginner error — FB70 will refuse the posting. Sirf naam-pata dalna kaafi nahi; FLCU00 + recon account ke baghair invoice post nahi hogi.

06 Reading the customer ledger — FBL5N & open items

The mirror of FBL1N: one screen that answers “who owes us what, and since when?”

Everything posted to a customer lands as a line item on the customer's account. Transaction FBL5N — Customer Line Item Display — is where you read that account (FBL1N was the vendor twin in Lesson 5, FBL3N the G/L twin in Lesson 4).

Key term · Open item

An open item is a line item that is not yet settled. A customer invoice is born as an open item (the customer still owes the money) and dies as a cleared item when a payment is matched to it. Open items are the heartbeat of AR: dunning, credit checks and ageing reports all read them. Open item = abhi tak ada na hua bill; paisay mil jayein to wohi item “cleared” ho jata hai.

How to read FBL5N

  1. Enter the customer number and company code.
  2. Choose what to see: Open items (at a key date), Cleared items, or All items — and the posting-date range.
  3. Execute. Each row is one document line: document number, type (DR invoice, DG credit memo, DZ payment), posting date, due date, amount, and a status symbol — open items and overdue items are visually flagged.
  4. Double-click any row to jump into the document itself (the same FB03-style display you know).
Key term · Baseline date & due date

The baseline date is the date from which the payment term starts counting — usually the invoice date. Baseline date + payment terms = due date. If the terms say “30 days net” and the baseline date is 1 November, the item is due on 1 December; from 2 December it is overdue and dunning can start. Baseline date woh tareekh hai jahan se udhaar ke din ginay jatay hain.

07 Step by step — FB70: enter a customer invoice

The AR twin of FB60: booking a sale on credit, directly in FI, with no sales order behind it.

Not every sale travels through the Sales (SD) module. A service charge, a rent-out of shop space, a manual correction — or simply the FI-side practice this course focuses on — is entered directly in FB70, the Enjoy screen for customer invoices. (Its classic twin is F-22, just as F-43 was FB60's twin.)

Key term · Posting keys for customers

Posting keys tell SAP what kind of line it is. For customers you need two: 01 = debit customer (the customer owes more — an invoice) and 11 = credit customer (the customer owes less — a payment or credit memo). On the G/L side you already know 40 = debit G/L and 50 = credit G/L. In FB70's Enjoy screen you do not type the customer keys — choosing “Invoice” or “Credit memo” sets them for you. 01 = customer debit (bill bana), 11 = customer credit (bill kam hua).

The FB70 steps

  1. Open FB70. The transaction type at the top is Invoice. Enter the company code if asked.
  2. Enter the customer number (e.g. 100045), the invoice date, the posting date, and the gross amount — the full amount the customer must pay, tax included (590,000 in our running example).
  3. SAP proposes the payment terms and baseline date from the customer master; check the resulting due date.
  4. In the line items, enter the revenue G/L account and the net amount, and a tax code if the sale is taxable (Section 16 explains tax codes; with a tax code, SAP calculates the tax line itself).
  5. Check the balance light turns green (debits = credits), then Post. SAP returns a document number — document type DR.

Exam trap · FB70 vs the SD billing document

In real life most customer invoices are created by the Sales module's billing run, which posts the FI document automatically. FB70 is the direct FI entry — used when there is no sales order/billing behind the invoice. The exam tests both facts: billing creates the invoice from SD; FB70/F-22 creates it manually in FI with document type DR. SD billing auto-invoice banati hai; FB70 haath se FI mein invoice hai.

08 FB75 — the customer credit memo

When the customer owes less than the invoice said — damaged goods returned, a price correction, a goodwill discount after the fact.

A credit memo on the customer side reduces what the customer owes. It is the exact mirror of the vendor credit memo (FB65) from Lesson 5. In AR the transaction is FB75 (classic twin F-27), document type DG.

Key term · Customer credit memo

A customer credit memo is a document that credits the customer — it lowers the receivable. Typical reasons: the customer returned damaged or wrong goods, you overcharged, or you grant a rebate afterwards. It is not a payment: no money moves; the open amount simply shrinks. Credit memo = bill mein kami ka parcha — paisay nahi aate, bas lena kam ho jata hai.

The entry

Scenario: City Style Garments returns a bundle of jackets with stitching defects, worth 59,000 gross (50,000 net + 9,000 tax). In FB75 you choose transaction type Credit memo, enter the customer and the gross amount, and the revenue line with the same tax code as the original sale:

LineAccountPosting keyDebitCredit
1Sales Revenue (or Sales Returns)4050,000—
2Output Tax (automatic — reverses part of the tax)409,000—
3Customer 10004511—59,000

Notice the direction: the customer line is a credit (key 11) — it reduces the debit balance the invoice created. The credit memo now sits on the customer account as a credit open item, waiting to be cleared against the invoice it belongs to (Section 11 shows that clearing in F-32).

Exam trap · Credit memo ≠ reversal

A credit memo is a new business document for a new business event (goods came back). It does not “undo” the original invoice. If the invoice itself was simply typed wrong, the correct tool is a reversal (FB08), not a credit memo. The exam separates these two ideas carefully. Ghalti hui ho to reversal; maal wapas aaya ho to credit memo.

09 Worked example — a sale, from invoice to open item

One complete story with real numbers. Every later section refers back to it.

The business event: On 1 November 2026, Ibrahim Apparel & Trading sells 500 winter jackets to City Style Garments (customer 100045). Net price: 500,000 PKR. Sales tax at 18% comes on top: 90,000 PKR. The customer pays within 30 days.

What SAP did, step by step

  1. FB70 posted document 1800000123, type DR: Dr Customer 590,000 · Cr Sales Revenue 500,000 · Cr Output Tax 90,000.
  2. The customer line updated the reconciliation account (Trade Receivables) automatically — the G/L now shows 590,000 more receivables, without anyone touching the G/L account.
  3. The invoice sits in FBL5N as an open item, due 01.12.2026 (baseline 01.11 + 30 days).
  4. On 20 November the customer returns damaged jackets: FB75 credit memo 1800000151, type DG for 59,000 gross — Dr Revenue 50,000, Dr Output Tax 9,000, Cr Customer 59,000.
  5. Net position in FBL5N: 590,000 − 59,000 = 531,000 still owed, shown as two open items waiting to be matched (Section 11 clears them together).
Analogy · Tax wala hissa amanat hai

Output tax ko kabhi apni kamai na samjhein. Customer se 590,000 aayenge, magar un mein se 90,000 sarkar ki amanat hai jo hum ne customer se collect ki hai — wohi raqam baad mein tax return mein jama hogi (Section 15 mein poori kahani). Revenue sirf 500,000 hai.

10 Step by step — F-28: incoming payment

The money arrives. F-28 books it into the bank and matches it against the customer's open items in the same step.

In AP you paid out with F-53; in AR you receive with F-28 — Post Incoming Payments. One F-28 document does two jobs at once: it records the money in the bank account, and it clears the open items the payment settles. Document type: DZ (customer payment).

The F-28 steps

  1. Open F-28. Enter the document date and posting date (the day the money hit the bank).
  2. In the bank data: the bank G/L account (or house bank account) and the amount received — 531,000 in our story.
  3. In the open-item selection: the customer account 100045. SAP lists the customer's open items: invoice 590,000 and credit memo −59,000.
  4. Activate both items. The assigned total (531,000) now equals the amount received — the “not assigned” difference shows zero.
  5. Post. SAP creates the DZ document and marks both items cleared, stamping them with the clearing document number and clearing date.
F-28 — Post Incoming Payments · Illustration (re-created for learning, not a real SAP screenshot)
Bank account 113000 Main Bank Account Amount received 531,000 PKR Value date 28.11.2026 Customer 100045 City Style Garments Open items selected DR 1800000123 Invoice ....... 590,000 DG 1800000151 Credit memo ... −59,000 Amount assigned 531,000 Not assigned 0 Resulting document (type DZ): Dr Bank 531,000 · Cr Customer 531,000 — both items cleared
Key term · Clearing (in F-28)

Clearing means marking open items as settled by matching them against each other so their net balance is zero. F-28 clears while it posts the payment — you do not need a separate clearing step when the payment matches exactly. Clearing = hisaab barabar karke items ko “settled” mark karna.

Exam trap · DZ vs KZ — direction letters

DZ = customer payment (money in, AR). KZ = vendor payment (money out, AP, from F-53/F110). And the invoice types pair up too: DR customer invoice, KR vendor invoice; DG customer credit memo, KG vendor credit memo. D-family = customer, K-family = vendor — the same D/K trick as the recon account types. D wala har document customer ka, K wala vendor ka.

11 Clearing customer open items — F-32

Sometimes items must be matched without any money moving. That is F-32's whole job.

F-32 — Clear Customer — is the AR twin of F-44 (Clear Vendor). It matches open items on a customer account against each other: an invoice against a credit memo, a payment posted “on account” against the invoice it later turns out to belong to, and so on. No bank line, no new money — just matching.

Key term · Payment on account

A payment on account is money received that is posted to the customer without matching it to a specific invoice yet — it sits as a credit open item. Later, when the right invoice is identified, F-32 clears the two against each other. On-account payment = paisay aa gaye, magar abhi kisi khaas bill se match nahi kiye.

The F-32 steps

  1. Open F-32, enter the customer and company code, and the clearing date.
  2. SAP lists all open items. Activate the items that belong together — for example an on-account payment of 59,000 against the credit memo DG of −59,000. The activated items must net to zero (items that leave a difference behind belong to the partial/residual cases in the next section).
  3. When the activated items net to zero, post. SAP stamps every selected item with the same clearing document number and clearing date; they move from “open” to “cleared” in FBL5N.
Exam trap · Clearing is not deleting — and FBRA undoes it

Cleared items are not deleted; they stay fully visible in FBL5N as cleared items, with their clearing document reference. If a clearing was done by mistake, you undo it with FBRA (Reset Cleared Items) — the items become open again. And remember from Lesson 5: a cleared document cannot be reversed with FB08 until its clearing has been reset with FBRA first. Clearing khatam nahi karti, sirf “settled” ka thappa lagati hai — FBRA woh thappa utaarta hai.

12 Partial payment vs residual payment

The customer pays less than the full invoice. SAP gives you two different ways to book it — and the exam tests the difference hard.

Story: the invoice is 590,000 (forget the credit memo for this section). The customer transfers only 400,000 now and promises the rest later. In F-28 you choose one of two tabs — Partial Payment or Residual Items. The cash received is identical; what happens to the open items is completely different:

Partial payment — invoice stays alive

  • The invoice stays open at the full 590,000.
  • The payment is posted as a separate credit open item of 400,000 on the same customer account.
  • FBL5N shows two open items; the balance owed reads 190,000.
  • The original due date of the invoice keeps driving dunning and ageing.

Bill poora khula rehta hai; payment apna alag item ban kar saath khari hoti hai.

Residual payment — invoice closes, leftover is born

  • The invoice is cleared (closed) by the payment.
  • SAP creates a new open item — the residual item — of 190,000, with its own document number and a fresh baseline/due date.
  • FBL5N shows one open item: the residual 190,000.
  • The leftover behaves like a small new invoice.

Bill band ho jata hai; baqi raqam aik naye chhote bill ki surat mein khuli rehti hai.

Memory trick · Partial = purana bill zinda, Residual = naya bill paida

Partial: purana bill zinda rehta hai, payment saathi item banti hai. Residual: purana bill mar jata hai (clear), baqi raqam ka naya bill (residual item) paida hota hai. Yehi farq AP mein bhi tha (Lesson 5) — dono taraf aik hi rule hai.

Exam trap · The balance is the same — the items are not

Both methods leave the customer owing 190,000, so balance-based questions cannot separate them. The exam asks about the items: “the invoice remains open in full” = partial; “the invoice is cleared and a new open item is created” = residual. Also note: a small difference can instead be written off as a payment difference (within tolerance) — that is a third path, where the difference goes to a difference account and everything clears. Balance dono mein aik hai — farq sirf items ki kahani mein hai.

13 Dunning — chasing overdue customers with F150

The due date passed and the money did not come. SAP's dunning program writes the reminders for you — politely first, firmly later.

Key term · Dunning

Dunning is the process of reminding customers about overdue open items — the reminder letters (or emails) a business sends when payment is late. In SAP the dunning run is transaction F150. Dunning does not post the receivable again; it works on the open items that already exist. Dunning = takaza — der se paisay dene walay customer ko baqaida reminder bhejna.

Key term · Dunning procedure & dunning levels

A dunning procedure (configured in FBMP, and stored in the customer's company code data) is the rulebook for reminders: how many dunning levels there are (commonly 3–4), how many days of delay trigger each level, what each letter says, and whether dunning charges or interest are added. Level 1 is a friendly note (“perhaps you forgot”); the last level is a final warning before legal steps. Every dunning run raises an item's level if it is still unpaid and enough days have passed. Dunning procedure = reminderon ka rulebook; har level pichle se zyada sakht hota hai.

How an F150 dunning run works

  1. Parameters: enter the run date, an identification, the company codes and the customers to include.
  2. Schedule the dunning run: SAP reads every open item of those customers, keeps the overdue ones, checks each customer's dunning procedure, and assigns or raises dunning levels. Items that must not be chased (e.g. blocked items) are skipped.
  3. Print / output: SAP produces the dunning notices from the run — one letter per customer, listing the overdue items, the level, and any dunning charges.
  4. The customer master and the items remember the last dunning date and level, so the next run continues the ladder instead of restarting it.
Key term · Dunning block

A dunning block stops dunning for a customer or a single item — for example while a complaint about damaged goods is being resolved. Blocked items are skipped by F150 even when overdue. Dunning block = “is customer ko filhal reminder na bhejo” wala flag.

Exam trap · Dunning needs three things in place

F150 produces nothing if (1) the customer master has no dunning procedure, (2) the items are not yet overdue (due date not passed, considering any grace days in the procedure), or (3) the items are dunning-blocked. And one more: dunning works on open items only — a paid (cleared) item can never be dunned. Procedure ho, item overdue ho, block na ho — teenon shartein lazmi hain.

14 Credit management & the Credit Control Area

Dunning reacts after payment is late. Credit management acts before the sale — it caps how much risk one customer may carry.

Selling on credit is a loan in disguise: you hand over goods today and trust the customer to pay next month. Credit management is SAP's system for keeping that trust within safe limits — every customer gets a maximum total exposure, and SAP watches the running total.

Key term · Credit Control Area

A Credit Control Area is the organisational unit in which credit limits are set and monitored (configured in OB45). One or more company codes are assigned to a credit control area, so a customer's exposure can be watched across all of them together. It has its own currency. Credit Control Area = woh unit jahan customers ki udhaar-had (credit limit) tay aur monitor hoti hai; kai company codes is se jur sakte hain.

Key term · Credit limit & credit exposure

The credit limit is the maximum amount of credit a customer may use — maintained in the customer's credit data (in the classic FI view with FD32; in S/4HANA Credit Management, in the BP role UKM000 per credit segment). The credit exposure is how much of that limit is currently used: open items + open sales orders + deliveries not yet billed. When a new order would push exposure over the limit, the credit check reacts — warning, or blocking the order/delivery until someone releases it. Limit = zyada se zyada udhaar; exposure = is waqt kitna udhaar chal raha hai.

Analogy · Mobile credit limit jaisa

Jaise mobile company aap ke number par aik credit limit lagati hai — limit khatam ho to mazeed calls ruk jati hain — bilkul waise hi SAP har customer ki limit lagata hai. Limit khatam, to naya order ruk jata hai jab tak paisay na aayein ya koi zimmedar shakhs order release na kare.

Exam trap · Credit Control Area is not a company code

The credit control area is its own organisational unit. Several company codes can share one credit control area (then the limit is watched across all of them), and the assignment is company code → credit control area. Do not confuse it with the controlling area from Part 1 — similar name, completely different job: controlling area = internal cost reporting; credit control area = customer credit risk. Controlling Area aur Credit Control Area — naam milta julta, kaam bilkul alag.

15 Taxes on Sales and Purchases — the big idea

Almost every invoice in this course quietly carried a tax line. Now we open that black box.

When a business sells goods, the government charges a tax on the sale — in Pakistan this is the sales tax (GST) collected under the FBR system; other countries call it VAT or GST too. The seller adds the tax to the price, collects it from the customer, and later hands it to the government. The same business also pays tax inside the prices of its own purchases. SAP calls this whole area Taxes on Sales/Purchases, and it has exactly two directions:

Output tax — tax on our SALES

  • Arises when we sell (FB70 invoice, SD billing).
  • We collect it from the customer inside the gross amount.
  • It is a liability: money owed to the government.
  • Posting: Cr Output Tax account.
  • Account key in SAP: MWS.

Output tax = hamari sale par tax — customer se liya, sarkar ko dena hai.

Input tax — tax on our PURCHASES

  • Arises when we buy (FB60 / MIRO vendor invoice).
  • We pay it to the vendor inside the gross amount.
  • It is a claim: money we may recover from the government.
  • Posting: Dr Input Tax account.
  • Account key in SAP: VST.

Input tax = hamari khareed par tax — vendor ko diya, sarkar se wapas lena hai.

The netting idea — why businesses only pay the difference

At the end of the tax period, the company does not hand over all the output tax it collected. It first subtracts the input tax it already paid on purchases. Only the difference goes to the government:

Key term · Non-deductible input tax

Some purchases carry tax that the law does not allow you to claim back — non-deductible input tax. That tax is not posted to the input tax account; it becomes part of the cost/expense itself. Whether input tax is deductible is decided by the tax code used on the line (next section). Har input tax wapas nahi milta — jo na mile, woh kharcha ban jata hai.

Exam trap · Tax is never revenue and never an expense (when deductible)

Deductible input tax is not part of your purchase cost, and output tax is not part of your sales revenue. Both live on balance-sheet tax accounts until they are netted and settled. If a question shows a purchase of 118,000 gross with 18% deductible tax, the expense is 100,000 — not 118,000. Tax P&L mein nahi jata — woh balance sheet ke tax khaaton mein rehta hai.

16 Tax codes — the recipe behind every tax line

You never type a tax amount by hand in a normal invoice. You type a two-character code — and SAP cooks the tax line from it.

Key term · Tax code

A tax code is a two-character code (per country) that bundles everything SAP needs to calculate tax for one situation: the tax type (input or output), the percentage rate (e.g. 18%), and which account key the tax posts through. You enter the tax code on the invoice line (FB70/FB60) or it is proposed from the master data — SAP then calculates the tax amount and creates the tax line automatically. Tax codes are defined per country / tax procedure in transaction FTXP. Tax code = tax ki mukammal recipe aik chhote se code mein — rate, qism, aur khaata.

Example tax codeDirectionRateUsed on
S1Output tax18%Sales invoices (FB70) — standard-rated sales
P1Input tax18%Purchase invoices (FB60/MIRO) — deductible
S0 / P0Output / Input0%Exempt or zero-rated transactions — posted with a code, but no tax amount
P2Input tax18%, non-deductiblePurchases whose tax becomes part of the cost

Codes above are teaching examples — real systems name their codes to match their own tax setup.

Key term · Tax category (in the G/L account master)

Whether a G/L account may carry tax at all is controlled in FS00 by the tax category field: “<” = only input tax allowed, “>” = only output tax allowed, “*” = all tax types allowed, blank = no tax postings to this account. A revenue account normally carries “>”, an expense account “<” or “*”. If the tax code's direction clashes with the account's category, SAP refuses the posting. Tax category = khaatay par kis qism ka tax lag sakta hai — ye FS00 mein tay hota hai.

Key term · Tax procedure & account key

Each country has a tax procedure — the calculation scheme listing the steps (base amount, rate, account key) tax follows in that country. Inside it, an account key (also called a transaction key) like MWS (output tax) or VST (input tax) marks where in the procedure the tax amount is calculated and through which key its G/L account will be found (Section 18, OB40). Tax procedure = mulk ka tax hisaab-framework; account key (MWS/VST) = us framework mein tax ki seat.

Exam trap · Tax codes belong to a country, not to a company code

A tax code is always defined for a country (the country of the tax procedure). Company codes in the same country share the same set of tax codes. Questions that say “the tax code is created per company code” are wrong. Also remember: the rate lives inside the tax code — you never maintain percentages on the invoice itself. Tax code mulk ke liye banta hai, company code ke liye nahi; rate code ke andar hota hai.

17 Step by step — FTXP: check a tax code

Before trusting a tax code on a live invoice, look inside it. FTXP is where tax codes are created and inspected.

  1. Open FTXP. SAP first asks for the country (e.g. PK) — because tax codes live per country.
  2. Enter the tax code you want to inspect, e.g. S1, and press Enter.
  3. Read the header: the code's description, its tax type (input or output tax), and whether it is active.
  4. Read the rate lines: each line shows an account key (MWS for output, VST for input), the percentage rate (e.g. 18.000%), and the level/step in the tax procedure where it applies.
  5. Check the properties: is the tax deductible? Is there an EU code or other reporting marker your country needs? For our check: S1 = output tax, 18%, account key MWS — exactly what FB70 used in Section 7.

Key term · Tax type A vs V

In FTXP, tax codes carry a tax type: A = output tax (Ausgang, sales side) and V = input tax (Vorsteuer, purchase side). SAP will not let you use an output code on a purchase line or an input code on a sales line — the direction is built into the code. A = output (sale), V = input (purchase) — code ki direction pehle se tay hai.

Exam trap · FTXP vs OB40 — recipe vs address

FTXP defines the tax code: rate and calculation. OB40 answers a different question: which G/L account receives the calculated amount. A very common exam pairing — if postings calculate the right tax but land on the wrong account, the fault is in account determination (OB40), not in the tax code. Next section shows OB40. FTXP = kitna tax; OB40 = kis khaatay mein jayega.

18 OB40 — telling SAP which G/L account receives the tax

The tax code calculates the amount. OB40 is the address book that says where that amount must be posted.

Key term · OB40 — tax account assignment

OB40 is the configuration transaction for automatic account determination for tax postings. For each account key / transaction key (MWS, VST, and others) you assign the G/L account the tax amount should post to, per chart of accounts. When FB70 calculates 90,000 of output tax through key MWS, SAP looks into OB40 and finds “MWS → Output Tax account 175000” — and posts there, without the user ever typing that account. OB40 = tax ki manzil ka pata — MWS ka tax kis khaatay mein, VST ka kis mein.

What you set in OB40

  1. Open OB40 and choose the transaction (account key) — e.g. MWS for output tax.
  2. Enter the chart of accounts of your company code.
  3. Assign the tax G/L account (e.g. 175000 Output Tax). Where rules differ by tax code, the assignment can be refined per tax code.
  4. Repeat for VST (input tax) and any other keys your tax procedure uses. Save — account determination is configuration, so it travels via a transport request.
Exam trap · The tax account itself must be tax-ready

The G/L accounts assigned in OB40 must be created in FS00 as balance sheet accounts with the right settings (and typically “posting without tax allowed” where needed). If the OB40 account is missing, the invoice posting fails with an account-determination error — the tax code and rate were fine. When a posting fails, check in this order: tax code exists (FTXP) → rate correct → OB40 account assigned → account exists in the company code. Posting ruke to pehle code, phir rate, phir OB40 ka khaata check karein.

19 Step by step — FB41: post tax on its own

Sometimes tax must be posted with no customer and no vendor in the document at all. FB41 exists for exactly that.

Normally tax lines are born inside invoices (FB70/FB60/MIRO). But real life produces tax-only situations: the tax authority's assessment adds tax for a past period, an import document carries tax that must be booked directly, or a correction must move tax between accounts. For these, SAP provides FB41 — Post Tax Payable — a screen that posts tax amounts directly, using posting keys for tax lines.

Key term · FB41 — Post Tax Payable

FB41 posts a document whose lines are tax lines and their offset — no subledger (customer/vendor) line is involved. You enter the tax code, and SAP proposes the tax account from OB40; the offsetting line goes to a G/L account you choose (for example a tax clearing or expense account). FB41 = sirf tax ki posting — na customer, na vendor, seedha tax khaata.

The FB41 steps (worked mini-example)

Scenario: a later tax assessment requires an extra 18,000 of output tax for a past period, to be charged against a tax expense/clearing account.

  1. Open FB41. Enter document date, posting date, company code and currency.
  2. First line: posting key for the offsetting G/L line — debit the tax clearing/expense account with 18,000, entering the relevant tax code on the line so SAP knows which tax this belongs to.
  3. SAP calculates and proposes the tax line: credit to the output tax account found through OB40 (key MWS).
  4. Simulate, check the document balances, and post. The tax now sits in the output tax account, ready to flow into the next tax return — with no customer invoice anywhere in sight.

Exam trap · FB41 is not “the tax version of FB70”

FB41 never creates a receivable or a payable — it cannot, because it has no subledger line. If a question describes tax arising from a sale to a customer, the answer is FB70 (or SD billing); FB41 is only for standalone tax postings such as assessments and corrections. Customer ke saath tax = FB70; akela tax = FB41.

20 Exam traps

The questions C_TS4FI loves to ask about this lesson — collected in one place.

Trap 1 · Customer creation in S/4HANA

Customers are created as Business Partners in BP — role FLCU00 for FI data, FLCU01 for sales data. XD01 still opens but works through CVI and creates a BP behind the screen. “Created” is not “postable”: without FLCU00 extended to the company code and a reconciliation account, FB70 refuses to post.

Trap 2 · The reconciliation account (AR edition)

The customer recon account lives in the company code data, is flagged in FS00 with recon account type D (vendor = K), is updated automatically with every customer posting, and can never be posted to directly. If a question offers “post the adjustment straight to the receivables recon account in FB50” — that option is always wrong.

Trap 3 · Document types and posting keys on the customer side

DR invoice (FB70/F-22), DG credit memo (FB75/F-27), DZ incoming payment (F-28). Posting keys: 01 debit customer, 11 credit customer. The vendor family mirrors it: KR, KG, KZ, keys 31/21. Mixing D and K families is the single most common slip.

Trap 4 · Partial vs residual — read the items, not the balance

Both leave the same amount still owed. Partial: original invoice stays open in full + payment is its own open item. Residual: original invoice is cleared + a new residual open item is created for the difference. Any question describing item behaviour is testing exactly this.

Trap 5 · Dunning preconditions

F150 duns only items that are open, overdue, and not dunning-blocked, for customers whose master carries a dunning procedure. Dunning raises levels per the procedure; it does not create the receivable and it never touches cleared items.

Trap 6 · Credit Control Area confusions

The Credit Control Area (OB45) is an independent organisational unit; company codes are assigned to it, and limits are monitored per customer across it. It is not the controlling area and not a company code. Credit exposure = open items + open orders + unbilled deliveries.

Trap 7 · Tax directions and addresses

Output tax (MWS) = on sales, a liability, credited. Input tax (VST) = on purchases, a claim, debited. The rate lives in the tax code (FTXP), which is defined per country; the account comes from OB40 via the account key. Deductible tax never touches the P&L; non-deductible input tax becomes part of the cost.

Trap 8 · FB41's limits

FB41 posts tax only — no customer line, no vendor line, no receivable, no payable. Tax that arises from a customer sale belongs in FB70 or SD billing.

22 Summary — the whole lesson on one screen

If you remember one screen before the exam, make it this one.

  • Accounts Receivable is the customer subledger under one G/L total. A customer in S/4HANA is a Business Partner (BP) with roles FLCU00 (FI / company code data) and FLCU01 (sales area data).
  • The customer account group (OBD2) controls number range, master-data field status and the one-time-customer flag. The reconciliation account (in the company code data; flagged “D” in FS00) mirrors every customer posting automatically — direct posting to it is impossible.
  • FBL5N shows customer line items. An open item is unsettled; due dates come from baseline date + payment terms.
  • FB70 = customer invoice without a sales order (type DR, keys 01/50). FB75 = customer credit memo (type DG, key 11 on the customer line) for returns and corrections — a business document, not a reversal.
  • F-28 books incoming payments (type DZ) and clears the matched items in the same document. F-32 clears items against each other with no money moving; a wrong clearing is reset with FBRA (before any FB08 reversal of a cleared document).
  • Partial payment: invoice stays open in full; payment is a separate open item. Residual payment: invoice is cleared; a new residual open item carries the difference.
  • Dunning (F150) reminds customers about open, overdue, unblocked items by dunning levels from the dunning procedure in the customer master (configured in FBMP).
  • Credit management: the Credit Control Area (OB45) is where customer credit limits are monitored; credit exposure = open items + open orders + unbilled deliveries; exceeding the limit triggers a warning or a block.
  • Output tax (MWS) is charged on sales — collected from the customer, owed to the government (credit). Input tax (VST) is paid on purchases — a claim when deductible (debit). The tax return settles only the difference. Deductible tax never touches the P&L.
  • Tax codes (FTXP) are defined per country and carry the rate and the account key. OB40 maps each account key to its tax G/L account per chart of accounts. FB41 posts tax on its own — no customer, no vendor.

01 Where Lesson 6 fits — the four rules of a company code

A quick recap of Lesson 5, then the map of this lesson (video 02:26:45 onwards).

In Lesson 5 you created a Company Code — one legal entity with its own books — and you saw its global parameters in OBY6: chart of accounts, fiscal year variant, field status variant, posting period variant. At that time we only named those settings. This lesson explains the four of them that the video covers next, in the video’s own order:

New term · Customizing

Almost everything in this lesson is Customizing — the settings a consultant makes in SPRO (the configuration menu, Lesson 5) to adapt SAP to one business, before any real postings happen. Customizing is not day-to-day work; end users never see it. Opposite of Customizing is master data (customers, vendors, G/L accounts) and transaction data (the postings themselves).

Customizing = system ki settings pehle se set karna. Master data = khaate aur parties. Transaction data = roz ki entries.

New term · Variant & the Variant Principle

A variant is a ready-made bundle of settings with a short name — like a saved “profile”. SAP’s variant principle says: define the bundle once, then assign it to as many company codes as you like. Fiscal Year Variant and Posting Period Variant both work this way. One change to the variant changes it for every company code using it — powerful, and a little dangerous.

Aik dafa setting banao, naam do, phir jitni company codes ko chahiye laga do — yehi variant principle hai.

Analogy · A new employee’s rule-book

Imagine a new accountant joins your company. Before his first entry, you tell him four things: (1) “Always write on the entry which department it belongs to.” (2) “Our year runs April to March, divided into 12 months.” (3) “Never let a customer’s total udhaar cross his approved limit.” (4) “Last month’s register is closed and locked — post only in the current month.” Those four instructions are this lesson — SAP just stores them as settings instead of telling a person.

02 Business Area — what it is and why it exists

Video 02:26:45 — the optional org unit we only named in Lesson 5.

Think of a real business you already know. One registered company — say Ibrahim Apparel & Trading — sells two very different product lines from the same legal entity: winter garments and home textiles. Legally it is one company: one tax registration, one Balance Sheet. So in SAP it is one company code.

But the owner keeps asking an internal question: “Which line is actually making money — garments or home textiles?” The company code alone cannot answer that, because inside one company code all postings mix together. The owner needs the books sliced by product line — without creating fake legal companies.

SAP’s classic answer to that slice is the Business Area.

New term · Business Area

A Business Area is an organisational unit that represents a separate area of operations or responsibilities inside a business — for example a product line, a division, or a line of business. It is used to draw up internal Balance Sheets and Profit & Loss statements for that area. Its code is up to 4 characters, and its technical field name is GSBER.

Business Area = aik hi company ke andar aik alag hissa ya product line, jis ka munafa aap alag dekhna chahte hain. Ye legal company nahi hoti.

Analogy · One shop, two counters

One shop (one company code), but two counters inside it: a garments counter and a home-textiles counter. Every sale slip is stamped with the counter’s name. At month-end the owner totals each counter separately: “Garments earned this much, textiles earned that much.” The stamp on the slip is the business area. The shop’s legal accounts stay one — the counters are only for the owner’s own understanding.

The three facts that define a Business Area

  • It is NOT a legal entity. It files no taxes, has no registration, and its Balance Sheet is for internal evaluation only — you cannot submit it to any authority.
  • It cuts ACROSS company codes. A business area is defined once for the whole client, not inside a company code. So “Garments” can exist in company code 1000 (Pakistan) and company code 2000 (UAE), and you can report garments across the whole group.
  • It is OPTIONAL. A company code posts perfectly well without any business area. You use it only if management wants that internal slice.
Memory trick

Company Code = legal line, Business Area = product line. Legal line → tax office cares. Product line → only the owner cares.

03 Business Area in detail — rules, limits & S/4HANA

Everything the exam can ask about the business area, in one place.

How does a posting GET a business area?

The business area is not typed on the document header — it is entered per line item (each debit/credit line can carry one, in field GSBER). In practice it usually arrives automatically:

  • From Logistics (MM/SD): you assign a business area to a Plant + Division combination in Customizing. When goods are sold or moved for that plant and division, SAP derives the business area by itself.
  • In pure FI postings: the user enters it on the line item, or it is filled by a default / substitution rule the consultant sets up.
  • On master data: some objects (for example cost centers) carry a business area, and postings to them inherit it.

Why its Balance Sheet is “internal only” — the honest limitation

You can produce a Balance Sheet and P&L per business area in SAP. But some balance sheet items cannot be split cleanly by division in real life: the bank account is shared, tax payable is for the whole legal company, a customer may buy from both divisions on one invoice. SAP therefore treats business-area financial statements as internal management reports, not legal statements. For a legally clean split you would need separate company codes.

Bank account aur tax poori company ka hota hai, aik product line ka nahi — is liye business area ki Balance Sheet sirf andar ke istemal ke liye hai.

Business Area vs Profit Center vs Segment S/4HANA favourite

In Lesson 5 you met the Profit Center (CO unit whose profit is measured separately). S/4HANA also has the Segment — a unit used for legally required segment reporting (reporting results by line of business or region in published accounts, under accounting standards like IFRS 8). All three can slice a company code — so which does the exam want?

New term · Document Splitting

Document Splitting is an S/4HANA FI feature that automatically splits each posting line by characteristics such as Profit Center and Segment, so that a complete, balanced Balance Sheet can be drawn up for each profit center / segment. (Full lesson on it comes later in this course.) Key point for today: business area is not a standard document-splitting characteristic — that is the technical reason S/4HANA prefers profit centers and segments for this job.

Exam trap preview

If a question says “management wants a balanced Balance Sheet per product line in S/4HANA”, the expected answer is Profit Center + Document Splitting (and Segment where segment reporting is meant) — not Business Area. Business area answers belong to questions about classic, internal, cross-company-code reporting.

04 Defining a Business Area (OX03), step by step

The actual Customizing work — it takes less than a minute, because a business area is just a code and a name.

Step by step

  • Step 1 — Open the setting. Either run transaction OX03 directly, or go through the menu: SPRO → Enterprise Structure → Definition → Financial Accounting → Define Business Area.
  • Step 2 — Click “New Entries”. You see an empty table with two columns: Business Area and Short Description.
  • Step 3 — Type the code and name. Code: up to 4 characters — e.g. GARM. Name: a plain description — “Garments Division”.
  • Step 4 — Save. SAP asks for a Customizing transport request (Lesson 2 — the parcel that carries settings DEV → QAS → PRD). Done. The business area now exists for the whole client — there is no separate “assign to company code” step, because it was never inside a company code.
  • Step 5 (for automatic derivation) — Assign to Plant/Division. In SPRO → Enterprise Structure → Assignment → Logistics – General → Assign Business Area to Plant/Division, link each plant + division pair to its business area, so MM/SD postings pick it up automatically.

05 Fiscal Year, Posting Periods & Special Periods

Video 02:32:00 — first the accounting idea, then the SAP setting. You already know this from Financial Accounting; SAP only gives it precise names.

In your accounting studies, a business does not report “from the beginning of time”. It cuts time into a financial year, prepares a Balance Sheet and P&L for that year, then starts fresh. SAP uses three precise terms for this cutting of time:

New term · Fiscal Year

The fiscal year is the 12-month accounting year of a company. It does not have to match the calendar year (January–December). Pakistan’s government fiscal year runs July–June; India’s runs April–March; the US federal government uses October–September. A company chooses the fiscal year its law and tax rules require. “Fiscal” simply means “relating to government finance / taxes”.

New term · Posting Period

A posting period is one slice of the fiscal year — normally one month. Every posting in SAP is booked into exactly one posting period, decided by its posting date. A normal fiscal year has 12 posting periods. Period-end reports (trial balance, P&L) are produced per posting period.

Posting period matlab woh mahina jisme entry record hogi — aam tor par saal ke 12 mahine, 12 periods.

New term · Special Periods

Special periods are up to 4 extra periods (13, 14, 15, 16) that sit after the last normal period of the fiscal year. They belong to the same fiscal year and the same calendar dates as the last month — they exist only so that year-end adjustment postings (audit corrections, final provisions, closing entries) can be kept separate from the normal business of the last month. Maximum in SAP: 16 periods in total = 12 normal + 4 special.

Special periods saal ke aakhir mein extra “pocket” hain — audit aur closing adjustments ke liye, taake aakhri mahine ke asal numbers kharab na hon.

Analogy · The diary with appendix pages

Your fiscal year is a diary with 12 monthly chapters. At the back, the diary has 4 blank appendix pages. After the year ends, the auditor finds mistakes — you do not rub out the December chapter; you write the corrections on the appendix pages, clearly labelled “year-end adjustments”. Those appendix pages are special periods 13–16. The year still has 12 months of real life — the appendix only holds corrections about that life.

06 Fiscal Year Variant — K4, V3, V6, V9 & OB29

The reusable calendar template of a company code.

New term · Fiscal Year Variant (FYV)

A Fiscal Year Variant is a named template that describes one kind of accounting calendar: how many posting periods the year has, how many are special periods, and on which calendar dates each period begins and ends. It is defined once (transaction OB29, table T009) and then assigned to company codes in their global parameters (OBY6, Lesson 5). Many company codes can share one variant — the variant principle again.

Fiscal Year Variant = saal ka calendar template: saal kab shuru hota hai, kitne periods hain, har period ki tareekhein kya hain.

The standard variants SAP delivers — learn these four

VariantFiscal year runsPeriodsTypical user

V3April – March12 + 4 specialIndia, UK, Japan

SAP delivers more (for example 24 — a year of 24 half-month periods), but K4 / V3 / V6 / V9 are the exam’s favourites. Notice the pattern: in every standard variant the period number follows the fiscal year, not the calendar month. In V3, April is period 1 and March is period 12 — even though March is calendar month 3.

Memory trick

K4 = “Kalender” 4 quarters — K is for the German word Kalenderjahr (calendar year), so K4 starts in January. For V3: “V3 — India’s April year, period 3 = June… ” simpler: just remember the exam pair — V3 = April–March. And V9: July–June is Pakistan’s government year.

Year-dependent vs year-independent

New term · Year-Dependent Variant

A variant is year-dependent if the calendar dates of its periods are different for different years — for example a retail calendar whose periods always end on a Saturday, so the dates shift every year. Then the consultant must maintain the exact period dates separately for each fiscal year (stored in table T009B). If the period dates are the same pattern every year (all four standard variants above), the variant is year-independent and needs no yearly maintenance. In OB29 a checkbox — “Year-dependent” — records which kind it is.

Shortened fiscal year

New term · Shortened Fiscal Year

A shortened fiscal year is a one-off fiscal year with fewer than 12 posting periods. It happens when a company changes its year-end (say from December to March — the transition year has only 3 months) or in a company’s first year of business. In the variant’s settings for that specific year, you reduce the number of periods and tick the “Shortened Fiscal Year” indicator, so SAP and the tax reports treat that stub year correctly.

Jab company apna saal ka aakhri mahina badalti hai, darmiyan wala saal chhota ho jata hai — wohi shortened fiscal year hai.

Defining and assigning — the two steps

  • Define (OB29): SPRO → Financial Accounting → Financial Accounting Global Settings → Ledgers → Fiscal Year and Posting Periods → Define Fiscal Year Variant. In practice you almost never build one from zero — you copy K4 or the closest standard variant, rename it (2 characters, e.g. Z1 — customer names start with Y or Z, Lesson 4), and adjust.
  • Maintain period dates (if year-dependent or non-standard): in the same OB29 screen, select the variant → Periods — enter each period’s month, day and year-shift (+/−1 tells SAP whether that month falls in the previous or next calendar year — e.g. in V3, January–March carry year-shift +1 because they belong to the next calendar year).
  • Assign (OBY6): SPRO → … → Assign Company Code to Fiscal Year Variant — or simply enter the variant in the company code’s global parameters. From Lesson 5 you know this is where it lives.

Exam trap preview · One variant, one rule

All company codes assigned to the same controlling area must use the same fiscal year variant (Lesson 5’s cross-company-code cost accounting rule). The exam loves combining these two lessons: change a company code’s FYV carelessly and CO breaks.

07 Credit Control Area — what it is and why it exists

Video 02:55:22 — the FI setting that protects you from customers who do not pay.

You know this from real business life. A wholesaler sells cloth to retailers on udhaar (credit): take the goods now, pay in 30 days. Credit increases sales — but every shopkeeper also knows the danger: give too much udhaar to one customer and, if he runs away, the loss can sink you. So every sensible business sets a rule: “This customer may owe us at most Rs 500,000 at any time. Not one rupee more.”

In SAP, the organisational unit that owns and enforces exactly this rule is the Credit Control Area.

New term · Credit Control Area (CCA)

A Credit Control Area is an organisational unit within which credit limits for customers are specified and monitored. Its code is up to 4 characters (table T014). One credit control area can cover one or many company codes — so a customer who buys from several of your legal companies gets one combined limit, checked against his total dues across all of them.

Credit Control Area = woh hissa jahan har customer ki udhaar limit set aur check hoti hai. Aik limit, saari company codes ke liye mili-juli.

New terms · Credit Limit & Credit Exposure

Credit limit = the maximum amount a customer is allowed to owe you at any moment — the ceiling. Credit exposure = how much the customer currently owes you in total, counting everything: unpaid invoices (open items), plus goods already ordered but not yet delivered, plus goods delivered but not yet invoiced. SAP’s rule is a single comparison, checked again and again: credit exposure must never exceed the credit limit.

Analogy · The shopkeeper’s udhaar khata

The old shopkeeper keeps one page per customer in his udhaar register. At the top of the page he writes the limit in red: “Max Rs 500,000”. Every new order is first added on the page — old unpaid bills, today’s order, the truck already loaded. If the total crosses the red line, he politely refuses: “Pehle purana hisaab clear karein.” SAP’s credit control area is that register, and the red line is checked automatically at every sales order, across every company code the register covers.

08 Credit Control Area in detail — OB45, OB38 & credit exposure

Define it, assign it, and see where the customer’s limit actually lives.

Step 1 — Define the credit control area (OB45)

  • Transaction OB45, or SPRO → Enterprise Structure → Definition → Financial Accounting → Define Credit Control Area.
  • Give it a code (up to 4 characters, often the same as the company code, e.g. 1000), a name, and its currency — all limits in this area are kept in that currency.
  • Two important settings live here: the update group and the default risk category (explained just below). Table: T014.
New terms · Risk Category & Update Group

A risk category is a label for how risky a customer is — e.g. “low risk”, “medium risk”, “high risk”. Different categories can get different strictness of credit check. An update group decides which values flow into the credit exposure — the standard update group 000012 adds up open orders + open deliveries + open billing documents + open items, exactly the four boxes in the diagram above.

Step 2 — Assign company codes (OB38)

  • Transaction OB38, or SPRO → Enterprise Structure → Assignment → Financial Accounting → Assign Company Code to Credit Control Area.
  • For each company code you set its default (permitted) credit control area. Several company codes may point to the same credit control area — that is the whole point: one shared limit per customer.
  • A company code can also be allowed to use other credit control areas for specific customers (“permitted credit control areas”), but it always has exactly one default.

Step 3 — The customer’s limit itself

The limit is not stored on the credit control area — it is stored per customer, per credit control area, in the customer’s credit master data. In classic SAP this was transaction FD32 (table KNKK). One customer can therefore have different limits in different credit control areas — generous in the area covering Pakistan, zero (cash only) in a new export area.

S/4HANA change · SAP Credit Management (FSCM) Exam favourite

In S/4HANA, classic credit management (FI-AR-CR, with FD32) is replaced by SAP Credit Management (part of FSCM — Financial Supply Chain Management). The credit limit now lives on the Business Partner (Lesson 1’s “one master record per party”) in the role “SAP Credit Management”, organised by credit segment — a unit much like a credit control area, and usually mapped from it. The credit control area is still defined in the enterprise structure (OB45/OB38) for compatibility, but the live credit check runs through credit segments and business partners. Exam questions on S/4HANA credit expect this answer.

What happens when the limit is crossed?

  • The credit check runs in SD (Sales & Distribution) when a sales order is created or changed — FI supplies the limit and exposure data. This FI–SD teamwork is called integration.
  • Depending on settings, SAP can show a warning, an error, or block the order / delivery until a credit manager releases it.
  • Payments received reduce open items → exposure falls → the customer can order again. The register updates itself.
Memory trick · OB45 / OB38

45 = “define” the area, 38 = “attach” the company codes. Or remember the flow of this whole lesson: OX03 business area → OB29 fiscal year → OB45 credit → OB38 assign credit → OB52 periods. Five codes, one lesson.

09 Posting Period Variant — the gatekeeper (OB52)

Video 03:10:00 — the setting that opens and locks the months.

Every month-end, accountants do something sacred: they close the month. Reports are printed, balances are agreed, tax is filed. After that, nobody may sneak a posting into that month — otherwise last month’s printed reports would silently stop matching the system. In manual accounting you would lock the register in a cupboard. In SAP, the lock is the Posting Period Variant.

New term · Posting Period Variant (PPV)

A Posting Period Variant is a named set of rules that decides, for each company code using it, which posting periods are open for posting and which are closed — separately for each account type. It is maintained in transaction OB52 (table T001B) and assigned to company codes in OBY6 — variant principle again: one variant can serve many company codes, so a group can open and close the month for all its companies with a single change.

Posting Period Variant = kis mahine mein entry ho sakti hai aur kis mein nahi — yehi system ka taala hai.

New term · Account Type

Every posting line belongs to one account type — the kind of account it hits. SAP uses one-letter codes, and you must know all six:

CodeAccount typeSimple meaning
+All account typesThe master row — applies to every type at once
AAssetsAsset accounting postings
DCustomersAccounts Receivable (Debtors)
KVendorsAccounts Payable (Creditors) — K from German Kreditor
MMaterialsStock / material postings from MM
SG/L accountsPure general-ledger postings

Why separate rows per account type? Because closing is staggered in real life: you may lock materials (M) postings on the 1st so stock is final, keep vendors (K) open two more days for late supplier invoices, and keep G/L (S) open longest for the accountants’ own adjustment entries. OB52’s per-type rows make exactly that possible.

The two period ranges — OB52’s cleverest idea

Each account-type row in OB52 has two “from period – to period” ranges, plus a column called AuGr (authorization group):

  • Range 1 (Period 1 columns) — the periods open for everybody with normal posting rights.
  • Range 2 (Period 2 columns) — extra periods open only for users who carry the authorization group named in the AuGr column — typically the senior accountants / closing team. This range is how special periods 13–16 are opened safely: normal users cannot touch them, the closing team can.
Analogy · The bank’s shutter and the manager’s cabin

At closing time the bank pulls the main shutter down — customers (normal users, Range 1) can no longer enter. But the manager and cashier, who have the cabin key (authorization group, Range 2), are still inside finishing the day’s balancing. OB52 works the same: one lock for the public, a second, key-holder-only range for the closing team — and that is where special-period adjustments are posted.

“Aunty D K M S” — Assets, Debtors (customers), Kreditors (vendors), Materials, Sachkonten (German for G/L accounts) — and + means “all of the above”. D and K are simply the English accounting words Debtors and K(C)reditors wearing German spelling.

10 Posting Period Variant in action — a posting’s journey

How the Fiscal Year Variant and Posting Period Variant work together on every single posting.

Step by step: what SAP checks when you press “Post”

  • Step 1 — Read the posting date. Every document has a posting date in its header — the date the entry belongs to (not necessarily today).
  • Step 2 — Date → period (Fiscal Year Variant’s job). SAP takes the company code’s fiscal year variant and asks: “Which fiscal year and which period does this date fall in?” Example with V3 (April–March): posting date 15 Feb 2027 → fiscal year 2026, period 11. (The fiscal year is named by the year in which it starts.)
  • Step 3 — Period open? (Posting Period Variant’s job). SAP takes the company code’s posting period variant and checks the row for the relevant account type: is period 11 / 2026 inside an open range? Yes → posting saved. No → hard error: “Posting period 011 2026 is not open” — the entry is refused completely; there is no “post anyway” button.
  • Step 4 — Special periods are the exception. A posting date can never “fall into” period 13–16, because those periods have no dates of their own. To post an adjustment, the user types a posting date inside the last normal period (e.g. 31 Mar 2027) and enters the special period number manually in the “Period” field of the document header. SAP then checks Range 2 and the user’s authorization group before allowing it.
Memory trick · Translator vs Gatekeeper

Fiscal Year Variant = translator (date → period number). Posting Period Variant = gatekeeper (period number → allowed or refused). If a posting fails with “period not open”, the gatekeeper (OB52) stopped it — the translator did its job fine.

The monthly closing routine

At each month-end the FI team edits OB52 for the company code’s variant: the finished month is removed from Range 1 (closed) and the new month becomes the open range. Because of the variant principle, if ten company codes share variant 1000, one OB52 change closes the month for all ten at once — convenient, and exactly why exam questions ask which company codes a PPV change affects: all company codes assigned to that variant.

One neighbour to know: Materials Management has its own period (the MM period, moved with MMPV) that controls goods movements. It is separate from the FI posting period — a favourite confusion in exam questions.

13 Exam traps

The mistakes C_TS4FI is designed to catch in this lesson’s four topics.

Trap 1 · Business area = legal entity

False. A business area has no legal standing; its financial statements are for internal evaluation only. Legal statements exist only at company code level.

Trap 2 · Business area is defined per company code

False. It is defined once at client level (OX03) and is valid across company codes — that cross-company view is its main purpose.

Trap 3 · A fiscal year can have 12 special periods

False. Maximum 16 periods total: 12 normal + at most 4 special (13–16). Any option claiming more is wrong.

Trap 4 · SAP finds the special period from the posting date

False. Special periods have no dates. The posting date must fall in the last normal period, and the user enters the special period manually in the document header’s Period field — plus needs the OB52 Range-2 authorization group.

Trap 5 · K4 starts in April

False. K4 = calendar year, January–December. April–March is V3. October–September is V6, July–June is V9. The exam shuffles these four relentlessly.

Trap 6 · One credit control area per company code, always

False. One credit control area can serve many company codes — that sharing is exactly how a customer gets one combined limit across the group. The company code side is the many; the CCA side can be one.

Trap 7 · Credit exposure = unpaid invoices only

False. With the standard update group 000012, exposure = open orders + open deliveries + open billing documents + open items. Forgetting the pipeline understates the customer’s true dues.

Trap 8 · OB52 assigns the variant to the company code

False. OB52 only maintains the open/closed intervals. The assignment of a posting period variant to a company code happens in OBY6 (global parameters) — same place as the fiscal year variant assignment.

Trap 9 · Account type “+” means G/L accounts

False. + means all account types. G/L accounts are S. Customers are D, vendors K, assets A, materials M.

Trap 10 · In S/4HANA, credit limits are still maintained in FD32

False for S/4HANA. Classic credit management is replaced by SAP Credit Management (FSCM): limits live on the Business Partner under credit segments. FD32/KNKK belong to the classic world the exam contrasts against.

Trap 11 · Changing one PPV affects one company code

False. Because of the variant principle, editing a posting period variant (or fiscal year variant) affects every company code assigned to it. Always check the assignment list before “just closing one company’s month”.

14 A–Z glossary

Every new term of this lesson, in one line each.

Account Type
One-letter kind of a posting line: + all, A assets, D customers, K vendors, M materials, S G/L accounts.
AuGr (Authorization Group)
OB52 column naming the group of users allowed to post in the second (restricted) period range.
Business Area
Optional, client-wide org unit for internal Balance Sheet / P&L per division or product line; max 4 characters; OX03; field GSBER; table TGSB.
Credit Control Area
Org unit within which customer credit limits are set and monitored; can cover many company codes; OB45 / OB38; table T014; field KKBER.
Credit Exposure
What a customer currently owes in total: open orders + open deliveries + open billing documents + open items.
Credit Limit
The maximum a customer may owe at any time; stored per customer per credit control area.
Credit Segment
S/4HANA (FSCM) unit on the Business Partner that holds credit limits — the successor idea to the credit control area.
Customizing
Consultant settings in SPRO that adapt SAP to a business before go-live; moved by transport requests.
Document Splitting
S/4HANA feature that splits posting lines by profit center / segment so balanced statements per unit are possible.
Fiscal Year
A company’s 12-month accounting year; need not equal the calendar year.
Fiscal Year Variant
Named calendar template: number of periods, special periods, and each period’s dates; OB29; table T009; field PERIV.
GSBER
Technical field name of the business area.
K4 / V3 / V6 / V9
Standard fiscal year variants: Jan–Dec / Apr–Mar / Oct–Sep / Jul–Jun — each 12 periods + 4 special.
OB29 / OB38 / OB45 / OB52
Define fiscal year variant / assign credit control area / define credit control area / maintain posting periods.
Open Items
Unpaid, uncleared lines on a customer or vendor account — part of credit exposure.
OX03
Transaction to define business areas.
Posting Date
The date in a document header that decides which fiscal year and period the posting belongs to.
Posting Period
One slice of the fiscal year, normally a month; a normal year has 12.
Posting Period Variant
Named rule-set deciding which periods are open or closed per account type; OB52; table T001B; assigned in OBY6.
Risk Category
Label of how risky a customer is; controls how strict the credit check is.
SAP Credit Management
The FSCM credit solution that replaces classic credit management in S/4HANA; limits on the Business Partner.
Segment
Reporting unit for segment reporting (e.g. IFRS 8), usually derived from the profit center; works with document splitting.
Shortened Fiscal Year
A one-off fiscal year with fewer than 12 posting periods, e.g. when a company changes its year-end.
Special Periods
Up to 4 extra periods (13–16) after the last normal period, for year-end adjustments; entered manually, never derived from a date.
Update Group
Setting deciding which document values flow into credit exposure; standard 000012 covers orders, deliveries, billing and open items.
Variant Principle
Define a settings bundle once, assign it to many company codes; a change affects all assignees.
Year-Dependent Variant
A fiscal year variant whose period dates differ per year and must be maintained for each year (T009B).

15 Summary — the whole lesson on one screen

  • Business Area (OX03, 4 chars, field GSBER, table TGSB): an optional, client-wide slice across company codes for internal Balance Sheet / P&L per division. Not a legal entity. In S/4HANA designs, Profit Center + Segment + Document Splitting are preferred for balanced per-unit statements.
  • Fiscal Year = 12-month accounting year; divided into 12 posting periods plus up to 4 special periods (13–16) for year-end adjustments — 16 maximum.
  • Fiscal Year Variant (OB29, table T009): the calendar template. K4 = Jan–Dec, V3 = Apr–Mar, V6 = Oct–Sep, V9 = Jul–Jun. Can be year-dependent; a shortened fiscal year has fewer than 12 periods. Assigned to company codes in OBY6.
  • Credit Control Area (OB45 define, OB38 assign, table T014): the unit that sets and monitors customer credit limits; one CCA can cover many company codes so a customer has one combined limit. Exposure = open orders + deliveries + billing + open items (update group 000012). In S/4HANA, SAP Credit Management (FSCM) with credit segments on the Business Partner replaces classic FD32 credit management.
  • Posting Period Variant (OB52, table T001B, assigned in OBY6): decides which periods are open or closed, per account type (+, A, D, K, M, S). Range 1 = everyone; Range 2 + AuGr = closing team only — that is how special periods are protected.
  • On every posting: FYV translates posting date → period; PPV guards whether that period is open; the credit check already guarded the sale in SD; the business area stamps the lines for internal reporting.

02 Asset Accounting — the big idea

A subledger for things that live longer than a year — with its own master records, its own postings, and a live bridge to the G/L.

When Ibrahim Apparel & Trading buys stitching machines for 2,400,000, that is not an expense of one month — the machines will earn money for years. Accounting spreads the cost over the machine's life. That spreading is depreciation, and the bookkeeping home for it is Asset Accounting, SAP module code FI-AA.

Key term · Fixed asset

A fixed asset (also called a capital asset) is a long-life thing the business owns and uses for more than one year — machinery, vehicles, buildings, computers, furniture. It sits on the balance sheet, not in one month's expenses. Fixed asset = lambi umar wali cheez jo business saalon istemal karta hai — machine, gaari, building.

Key term · Asset Accounting (FI-AA) & the asset subledger

Asset Accounting is a subledger of FI, exactly like AP and AR. AP keeps detail per vendor, AR per customer — FI-AA keeps detail per asset: cost, accumulated depreciation, net book value, location, useful life. And like AP/AR, it is tied to the G/L by reconciliation accounts: you never post to the asset G/L accounts directly; every asset posting updates them automatically. FI-AA = assets ka tafseeli register; G/L mein sirf total, woh bhi khud-ba-khud.

Key term · APC, accumulated depreciation & net book value

APC means Acquisition and Production Costs — simply: what the asset cost when you got it. Accumulated depreciation is the total value lost so far. Net book value (NBV) = APC − accumulated depreciation: what the asset is still “worth on the books” today. Example: machine APC 2,400,000, depreciation so far 600,000 → NBV 1,800,000. NBV = asal qeemat − ab tak ki depreciation.

Key term · Capitalisation

Capitalisation means putting a purchase onto the balance sheet as an asset instead of expensing it at once. The posting that does this is called an asset acquisition. Small cheap items (a 3,000 mouse) are usually expensed directly; valuable long-life items are capitalised. Many companies set a capitalisation threshold — a minimum value — in policy. Capitalise karna = kharche ko foran expense na likhna, asset bana kar balance sheet mein rakhna.

Analogy · Har machine ki apni file

Sochain godam mein har machine ke liye ek file hai: khareed ki raseed (APC), har saal kitni purani hui (depreciation), ab kitni qeemat bachi (NBV), machine kahan khari hai (location/cost center). FI-AA wohi file cabinet hai — aur G/L sirf cabinet ke bahar lagi hui kul-qeemat ki parchi hai, jo har file ki tabdeeli se khud update hoti hai.

03 Chart of depreciation & depreciation areas

One asset, several rule-books: tax law, company reporting and group reporting may each value the same machine differently.

Pakistan tax rules may allow a machine to be written off in 5 years, while the company's own books use 8 years, and the group (parent company) uses 10. SAP does not force a choice — it values the same asset in parallel, once per rule-book. Each rule-book view is a depreciation area, and the full set of areas for a country is the chart of depreciation.

Key term · Chart of depreciation

A chart of depreciation is the country-level bundle of depreciation areas and rules, assigned to a company code. SAP delivers country templates (you copy and adjust). It is to assets what the chart of accounts is to G/L accounts — a standard framework the company adopts. Note: despite the similar name, it is a different object from the chart of accounts. Chart of depreciation = aik mulk ke liye depreciation ke usoolon ka set, company code ko assign hota hai.

Key term · Depreciation area

A depreciation area is one valuation view of all assets — for example area 01 Book depreciation (the real one that posts to the G/L), area 15 Tax depreciation (for the tax return, usually values-only), or a group area. Each area has its own depreciation key, useful life and values for the same physical asset. Depreciation area = aik hi asset ko aik khaas usool (book / tax / group) se dekhna.

Key term · Real area vs derived area

Area 01 is a real area — its values are stored and posted to the G/L. Some areas are derived: they are calculated on the fly as the difference of two real areas (e.g. book − tax) and store nothing themselves. In S/4HANA with the Universal Journal (ACDOCA, Part 1), each posting area is typically tied to a ledger — area 01 posts to the leading ledger 0L. Exam favourite Real area = asal values store hoti hain; derived area = do areas ka farq, khud calculate hota hai.

Key term · Depreciation key & useful life

The depreciation key is the calculation recipe: e.g. straight-line (equal amount every year) or declining balance (bigger amounts early). Useful life is how many years the asset will serve. Straight-line on 2,400,000 over 8 years = 300,000 per year, 25,000 per month. Depreciation key = hisaab ka tareeqa; useful life = asset kitne saal chalega.

Exam trap · Chart of depreciation ≠ chart of accounts

Two different “charts”. The chart of accounts lists G/L accounts (OB13). The chart of depreciation lists depreciation areas and is assigned per company code. Also remember: a company code can have only one chart of depreciation, but a chart of depreciation can serve many company codes in the same country. Naam milta-julta hai, cheez bilkul alag hai — imtihan ki pasandeeda chaal.

04 Asset classes — OAOA

The asset class is the master template: it decides the G/L accounts, the number range and the default depreciation rules for every asset of that kind.

Before you can create a single asset, its asset class must exist. Classes are the families: Machinery, Vehicles, Buildings, IT Equipment, Furniture, and the special Assets under Construction. Asset classes are defined and viewed in transaction OAOA.

Key term · Asset class

An asset class groups assets of the same kind and controls, for every asset created in it: (1) the account determination — which G/L accounts receive APC, depreciation and retirement postings; (2) the number range for asset numbers; (3) default depreciation areas, keys and useful life; (4) which fields appear on the asset master screen. Asset class = assets ka khandan; accounts, number aur depreciation ke defaults yahin se aate hain.

Key term · Account determination (assets)

For assets, account determination is the link inside the asset class that names the G/L accounts: the balance-sheet APC account, the accumulated depreciation account, and the depreciation expense account (a P&L account, usually charged to a cost center). When AFAB runs, it knows where to post because the class told it. Account determination = class ke andar likha hua “kaunsa G/L account kahan lagega”.

Key term · Asset under Construction (AuC)

An Asset under Construction collects the costs of something being built (a new warehouse, a machine being assembled). While under construction it is not depreciated. When finished, its collected costs are settled / transferred to the final asset, and depreciation starts. AuC = ban raha asset; mukammal honay tak depreciation nahi lagti.

Memory trick · Class = saanche (mould)

Asset class ek saancha hai: jaisa saanca, waisi murti. Har naya asset apni class se accounts, number range aur depreciation defaults “inherit” karta hai — is liye AS01 mein zyada tar fields pehle se bhare milte hain.

05 Step by step — AS01: create an asset master (AS02 / AS03)

The asset master is the asset's file: description, location, cost center, useful life — created before any money is posted to it.

Scenario: the company will buy a delivery van. First the master, then the purchase. The family of transactions: AS01 create, AS02 change, AS03 display — the same 01/02/03 rhythm you know from FS00 and BP-related work.

  1. Open AS01. Enter the asset class (e.g. Vehicles) and the company code. SAP takes the next number from the class's number range.
  2. General tab: description (“Delivery Van — Suzuki Bolan”), quantity/unit if relevant.
  3. Time-dependent tab: the cost center that will bear the depreciation (e.g. Logistics), plus location / room. “Time-dependent” means these assignments can change over time with validity dates — the van can move from one department to another.
  4. Depreciation areas tab: for each area, confirm the depreciation key and useful life inherited from the class (e.g. straight-line, 5 years) and the depreciation start date logic.
  5. Save. SAP confirms the asset number, e.g. 400012. At this moment the asset exists but its value is still zero — no acquisition has been posted yet.

Key term · Asset number & subnumber

Every asset has a main number (e.g. 400012). Parts added later — a new engine for the van — can be tracked as subnumbers (400012-1, 400012-2) under the same main asset, each with its own cost and life. Subnumber = aik hi asset ke hissay, alag hisaab ke saath.

Exam trap · AS01 creates no value

Creating the master posts nothing to the G/L. The balance appears only with an acquisition posting (next section). A favourite exam picture: “Asset created in AS01 — what is its book value?” Answer: zero. File ban gayi, magar file abhi khaali hai.

06 Step by step — F-90: asset acquisition (and ABSO)

Now the money: buying the van from a vendor and capitalising it in one document.

The van costs 3,000,000 from a dealer. The classic FI way to book a purchase with a vendor is F-90 — Acquisition from Purchase with Vendor. Its posting keys are exam material:

  1. Open F-90. Document type is typically KR-style vendor logic — in practice the standard asset acquisition with vendor uses posting keys below.
  2. First line: posting key 31 (credit vendor) — enter the vendor, amount 3,000,000.
  3. Second line: posting key 70 (debit asset) — enter asset number 400012 and transaction type 100 (external acquisition). Amount 3,000,000.
  4. Simulate, check, and post. One document: Cr Vendor 3,000,000 / Dr Asset (via subledger) 3,000,000. The asset's APC is now 3,000,000 and the G/L asset account was updated automatically through the subledger.
Key term · Posting keys 70 / 75 & transaction type

Posting key 70 = debit an asset, 75 = credit an asset. Every asset posting also carries a transaction type — a code saying what kind of movement this is (100 = acquisition from purchase, 200 = retirement, 300-series = transfers…). Reports and the asset history sheet group movements by transaction type. 70 = asset debit, 75 = asset credit; transaction type batata hai ke harkat kis qism ki hai.

Key term · ABSO — miscellaneous posting

ABSO is the general asset posting screen used when the classic Enjoy transactions do not fit — e.g. an acquisition without a vendor (offset to a clearing or bank account), or special adjustments. F-90 is simply the vendor-flavoured specialist; ABSO is the flexible all-rounder where you choose the transaction type yourself. ABSO = asset posting ka general screen, jab vendor wala rasta na ho.

Key term · Integrated vs non-integrated acquisition

Our F-90 is integrated: asset and vendor are posted in the same document, so AP and AA can never disagree. (The purchasing route — PO, goods receipt, MIRO — also capitalises the asset through the same integration.) A non-integrated acquisition posts the asset first against a clearing account and settles the vendor side separately. Integrated = asset aur vendor aik hi document mein.

Exam trap · The asset G/L account is never typed

In F-90 you enter the asset number, never the asset G/L account. SAP finds the account through the asset class's account determination — and, as with AP/AR recon accounts, direct posting to asset balance-sheet accounts is blocked. Asset number likho, G/L account nahi — woh class se khud aata hai.

07 Transfer with ABUM & retirement with ABAVN

Moving value between assets, and the end of the road: scrapping or selling an asset.

ABUM — transfer within the company

A transfer moves an asset (or part of its value) to another asset — e.g. the AuC warehouse is finished and its 18,000,000 of collected cost moves to the new Buildings asset, or one machine is split between two cost centers via two assets. Transaction ABUM posts the transfer: the sending asset is credited, the receiving asset debited, with the asset's history (APC and accumulated depreciation) moving along — or, if you choose, only current-year values. ABUM = aik asset se doosray asset mein value muntakil karna — company ke andar hi.

Key term · Transfer & gross / net method

With the gross method, APC and accumulated depreciation move separately, so the receiving asset keeps the full history. With the net method, only the net book value moves and the receiver looks “new”. The method is set in the transfer's transaction type / transfer variant. Gross = poori tareekh saath jati hai; net = sirf bachi hui qeemat.

ABAVN — retirement by scrapping

Five years later the van is finished — engine dead, sold as scrap for nothing. Retirement removes the asset from the books. ABAVN is retirement by scrapping (no revenue): SAP removes the APC (3,000,000) and the accumulated depreciation (say 2,700,000), and the remaining book value (300,000) is posted as a loss on retirement to the P&L. After retirement the asset is deactivated — no more depreciation, no more postings except history display.

Key term · Retirement with revenue (sale)

Selling an asset instead uses F-92 (retirement with customer) or ABAON (retirement without customer, revenue typed in). SAP compares the sale price with the net book value: price above NBV = gain, below = loss — posted automatically. Bechna = F-92 / ABAON; qeemat NBV se zyada to munafa, kam to nuqsan.

Exam trap · Retirement ≠ deletion

A retired asset is not deleted and its documents are not reversed. It stays visible for years of reporting (asset history sheet) with value zero. Deleting an asset master (possible only for assets with no postings) is a completely different act. Retire = hisaab band, tareekh mehfooz; delete nahi hota.

08 Step by step — AFAB: the depreciation run

Depreciation is never typed by hand. Once a period, one program calculates and posts it for every asset at once.

The van: APC 3,000,000, straight-line, 5 years → 600,000 per year → 50,000 per month. Nobody posts 50,000 manually for hundreds of assets. Instead, at period end, you run AFAB — the depreciation posting run.

  1. Open AFAB. Enter company code, fiscal year and posting period.
  2. Choose the run type: Planned posting run (the normal monthly run), Repeat run (redo a period, e.g. assets acquired later), Restart run (resume a run that stopped with an error), or Unplanned run (an extra run outside the plan).
  3. First execute as a test run — SAP calculates everything and lists totals and errors, but posts nothing. Fix any errors (e.g. a missing cost center on a new asset).
  4. Execute the productive (real) run, usually as a background job. SAP posts one summarised document per run: Dr Depreciation expense 50,000 (to the asset's cost center) / Cr Accumulated depreciation 50,000 — for all assets together, per account.
  5. Check the run's log and the posted document. The period is then marked as posted for depreciation — a normal planned run for the same period cannot simply be repeated; that is what the repeat run is for.
Key term · Depreciation posting run (AFAB)

The depreciation run is a period-end program that calculates depreciation for all assets from their keys and useful lives, and posts it in batch to the G/L (expense + accumulated depreciation), using the asset class account determination and each asset's cost center. It posts summarised, not asset-by-asset documents — the per-asset detail lives in FI-AA itself. AFAB = mahina khatam par tamam assets ki depreciation aik saath calculate aur post karne wala program.

Exam trap · AFAB is not a manual journal

You cannot “just FB50” depreciation and expect the subledger to agree — the asset's own depreciation values would be wrong forever. Depreciation must come through AFAB (or asset transactions) so FI-AA and the G/L stay reconciled. Also: a run that ends with errors must be continued with a restart run, not a fresh planned run. Depreciation haath se nahi, AFAB se — aur adhoora run restart se poora hota hai.

09 AW01N — the Asset Explorer

One screen that answers every question about one asset: what it cost, what happened to it, what it will still depreciate.

Transaction AW01N, the Asset Explorer, is the display heart of FI-AA. Give it an asset number and a fiscal year, and it shows, per depreciation area:

  • Planned values: APC, accumulated depreciation, NBV — opening, movements, closing — plus the depreciation planned for each future period.
  • Posted values: what AFAB has actually posted so far.
  • Transactions tab: every document that touched the asset — acquisition, transfer, retirement — and you can drill into the FI document.
  • Comparison: side-by-side values of two depreciation areas (book vs tax) — the parallel valuation of Section 3 made visible.

10 Bank Accounting — the big idea

Cash is the easiest thing to lose track of — so SAP gives every bank account its own structure and a strict reconciliation routine.

Bank Accounting (FI-BL) manages the company's bank accounts inside FI: payments out, receipts in, cheque management, and above all bank reconciliation — proving that SAP's bank balance equals the real bank's balance. The difference between the two, at any moment, should be only timing: cheques issued but not yet presented, deposits recorded but not yet credited.

Key term · Bank reconciliation & clearing accounts

To keep timing differences visible, SAP routes bank movements through bank clearing (transit) accounts: e.g. a payment first lands on “Bank Clearing — Payments Out” and only moves to the main bank account when the bank statement proves the money actually left. Open items sitting in clearing accounts = exactly the timing differences. Clearing account = darmiyani khata; paisa asal bank account mein tab jata hai jab statement se sabit ho jaye.

11 House bank (FI12) & the bank G/L account

Three levels, always in this order: house bank → bank account → G/L account.

Key term · House bank

A house bank is a bank where your company holds accounts — e.g. “Meezan Bank” as seen by your company code. It is defined in transaction FI12 (in S/4HANA, FI12_HBANK / the Fiori app Manage Banks) with a short house bank ID (e.g. MEEZ). Do not confuse it with a bank master record (transaction FI01) — the general directory of all banks in the country, used for vendors' and customers' banks too. House bank = woh bank jahan HAMARA account hai; bank master = tamam banks ki directory.

Key term · Bank account & account ID

Under each house bank sit its bank accounts, each with an account ID (e.g. CUR01 — current account), the real account number / IBAN, the currency, and — the key link — the G/L account that represents this bank account in the books. Payment programs (F110, Lesson 5) find the money through exactly this chain: house bank + account ID → G/L account. Account ID = house bank ke andar har account ka chhota naam, jo G/L account se jurta hai.

House banks are defined for a company code — two company codes banking at the same Meezan branch each define their own house bank entry. 12 Step by step — F-58: payment with cheque print

The manual cousin of F110: pay one vendor now, and print the cheque in the same step.

In Lesson 5 you paid vendors manually with F-53 (no cheque) and in bulk with F110. F-58 — Post Payment with Printout — is the manual payment that also prints the cheque / payment advice immediately, using the house bank's cheque lot.

  1. Open F-58. Enter company code, payment method C (cheque), house bank and account ID, and the cheque lot / printer form.
  2. Enter the vendor and amount, then process the open items — select the invoice(s) this cheque settles (same open-item selection skill as F-53 / F-28).
  3. Post and print. SAP posts Dr Vendor / Cr Bank Clearing — Outgoing Cheques (document type KZ), assigns the next cheque number from the lot, and prints the cheque.
  4. The cheque number is now linked to the payment document — traceable in the cheque register (FCHN). If a cheque is spoiled, it is voided (FCH3), never deleted, so the number sequence stays complete for audit.
Key term · Cheque lot

A cheque lot is a numbered bundle of cheque leaves assigned to a house bank account (e.g. lot 1 = cheques 000101–000200). SAP tracks every number: used, void, or unused — auditors love a complete sequence. Cheque lot = number-daar cheques ki gaddi; har number ka hisaab rehta hai.

Exam trap · F-58 vs F-53 vs F110

F-53 = manual payment, no print. F-58 = manual payment with cheque print. F110 = automatic program for many payments. Same KZ result, three doors. 53 = baghair print, 58 = cheque print ke saath, F110 = automatic.

13 FF67 bank statement & F-03 bank clearing

The bank speaks — through the statement. SAP listens, matches, and moves money from clearing to the main account.

FF67 — enter the bank statement manually

FF67 is the manual bank statement entry: you type the statement the bank sent (opening balance, each line, closing balance). Each line carries a transaction / business transaction code (posting rule) that tells SAP what the line is — a presented cheque, a bank charge, a customer deposit — and how to post it. Where banks send files, the same statement arrives electronically (FF_5 import, formats like MT940 / CAMT) — the posting logic is identical.

  1. Open FF67, enter house bank, account ID, statement number and date.
  2. Type the opening balance (must equal the last statement's closing balance) and each statement line: amount, value date, transaction code, reference (e.g. cheque number).
  3. Enter the closing balance. SAP checks: opening + lines = closing. Mismatch = the statement cannot be saved as balanced.
  4. Post the statement. For a presented cheque, SAP posts Dr Bank Clearing — Outgoing Cheques / Cr Main Bank Account — the money now officially leaves the main bank account in SAP too.

F-03 — clear the G/L clearing account

The statement posting creates a matching line in the clearing account. F-03 (Clear G/L Account) is where you clear those pairs: select the clearing account, match the original payment line (from F-58) with the statement line (from FF67) — equal and opposite — and clear them. What remains open in the clearing account is, by definition, your unreconciled timing differences. (Automatic clearing program F.13 can do this matching in bulk using rules like cheque number / amount.)

Key term · Value date

The value date is the date from which the bank counts the money as in/out (interest and balance purposes). It can differ from the posting date — a cheque deposited Friday may carry Monday's value date. Statements and clearing care about value dates. Value date = woh tareekh jis se bank paisa asal mein ginna shuru karta hai.

Exam trap · Clearing is not posting

F-03 creates no amounts — it only stamps existing open items as cleared and sets the clearing document/date. If the two sides differ, F-03 will not “fix” it; differences must be posted properly (e.g. bank charges via the statement's posting rules). F-03 sirf jorta (match) hai, raqam nahi banata.

14 Cost Center — KS01

Controlling (CO) asks the question FI alone cannot answer: whose cost is this?

FI tells you the company spent 50,000 on depreciation. Management asks: which department? That “which department / which unit” label is the cost center — the basic object of Controlling (CO), SAP's internal (management) accounting. Cost centers are created in KS01, changed in KS02, displayed in KS03.

Key term · Cost center & controlling area

A cost center is an organisational unit where costs are collected and whose manager is responsible for them — Production, Logistics, Admin, Sales Office. Cost centers live inside a controlling area: the CO counterpart of the company code level, to which one or more company codes are assigned. An FI posting of a cost/revenue line to a primary cost element account must carry a CO object — usually a cost center — or SAP refuses to post. That is why the van's depreciation needed “LOG-100 Logistics” in Section 5. Cost center = kharcha kis department ka hai; baghair is ke cost posting ruk jati hai.

  1. Open KS01. Enter the controlling area and the new cost center code (e.g. LOG-100) and its validity dates.
  2. Enter name, description, person responsible, cost center category (e.g. production, administration, sales) and the hierarchy area — where it sits in the standard hierarchy, the tree that rolls all cost centers up to the company total.
  3. Set control indicators if needed (e.g. lock against postings). Save.
Key term · Cost element

In S/4HANA, P&L accounts that carry costs/revenues into CO are called cost elements — and they are simply G/L accounts whose master (FS00) marks them with an account type / cost element category. One chart of accounts serves both FI and CO — a big S/4HANA simplification. Cost element = woh P&L G/L account jo kharcha CO tak le jata hai.

15 Profit Center — KE51

Cost centers collect costs. Profit centers answer the bigger question: did this part of the business make money?

Key term · Profit center

A profit center is a unit of the business viewed as its own mini-company: it collects both revenues and costs, so you can produce a mini profit & loss — and with document splitting (Part 1), even a mini balance sheet — for it. Examples for Ibrahim Apparel & Trading: “Winter Wear”, “Formal Shirts”, “Online Sales”. Profit centers are created in KE51, changed in KE52, displayed in KE53, inside a controlling area. Profit center = business ka woh hissa jiska apna munafa-naqsan nikala jata hai.

How does a posting get its profit center? Usually automatically: each cost center is assigned to one profit center, so a posting to LOG-100 silently carries profit center “Logistics” with it. Materials, assets and other objects have their own assignments. That automatic derivation is why profit center reporting stays complete without users typing it on every line.

Key term · Dummy profit center

If a posting reaches CO with no derivable profit center, it falls into the dummy profit center — a warning bucket. A growing dummy balance means master-data assignments are broken somewhere. Good month-end hygiene: check the dummy profit center is (near) zero. Dummy profit center = “kahin na kahin galti hai” wala khata — is ka balance zero hona chahiye.

Exam trap · Profit center ≠ profit center accounting as a separate ledger

In S/4HANA, profit center values are not kept in a separate side-table world: they are fields in the Universal Journal (ACDOCA), posted in the same document as FI. Classic “PCA” as a separate ledger is history. S/4HANA mein profit center ACDOCA ke andar hi rehta hai — alag ledger nahi.

16 Internal Order — KO01

Cost centers are forever. Projects are not — for one-off jobs, CO uses the internal order.

Key term · Internal order

An internal order is a temporary CO object that collects the costs (and sometimes revenues) of one defined job or event: “Exhibition Stall — Lahore Expo”, “Machine Overhaul 2026”, “New Shop Fit-Out”. It is created in KO01 (change KO02, display KO03), given a budget if needed, charged by real postings, and at the end its collected costs are settled — moved on to a cost center, an asset (classic for AuC!), or a G/L account — after which the order is closed. Internal order = aik khaas kaam/project ka kharcha ikatha karne wala temporary khata; aakhir mein settle ho kar band ho jata hai.

Key term · Real vs statistical posting

A posting can carry only one real CO object (the one that truly bears the cost — e.g. an internal order). Extra objects can ride along statistically — recorded for reporting, but not the true cost bearer. Example: Dr Repair expense — real: Internal Order “Overhaul 2026”; statistical: the Production cost center. Real = asal zimmedar aik hi; statistical = sirf report ke liye saath likha hua.

Memory trick · KS, KE, KO — teeno “01”

KS01 cost center (S = Stelle, German for “place”), KE51 profit center, KO01 internal order (O = Order). Aur yaad rahe: order ka kharcha aakhir mein settle hota hai — yehi us ki pehchan hai.

17 Period-end closing & OB52 — open and close posting periods

Closing is a sequence, not a single button. Its gatekeeper is the posting period control.

Period-end closing is the ordered set of jobs that finishes a month (or year): run depreciation (AFAB — done above), value foreign currency (next section), post accruals, reconcile subledgers, then lock the period so nothing can sneak into a finished month. The lock itself lives in OB52 — the transaction that opens and closes posting periods, working on the posting period variant you configured back in Part 2 · Lesson 2.

Key term · OB52 — open/close periods

In OB52, for each posting period variant + account type (+, A assets, D customers, K vendors, M materials, S G/L), you set the range of periods open for posting: “from period / to period”, with a second range and an authorisation group that can let a small closing team post into an otherwise closed period (level 3 periods). Normal users get hard errors like “Period 004/2026 is not open”. OB52 = kis period mein posting allowed hai, is ka darwaza; band period = taala laga darwaza.

  1. Finish the period's operational postings; run AFAB and the other closing programs (Sections 18–20).
  2. Open OB52, select the posting period variant, and shift the open range forward: close period 4 by moving “from period” to 5 for all account types.
  3. Keep the special authorisation range for the closing team until final adjustments are done, then remove it too.
  4. At year-end, remember the special periods 13–16 (Part 1): closing adjustments are posted into these adjustment periods of the old year while the new year is already open for daily business.
Exam trap · OB52 controls FI posting periods — not everything

OB52 governs FI documents. Materials movements have their own period control (MMPV), and CO has its own locks (OKP1). Also: closing a period never deletes or moves documents — it only refuses new postings with posting dates in that period. Band karna = nayi posting rokna; purani documents waisi ki waisi rehti hain.

18 Foreign currency valuation — F.05

A dollar receivable booked at 278 is worth something else at month-end. Valuation corrects the books — temporarily.

Part 1 taught exchange rates and currency translation. At period end, open items and balances in foreign currency must be re-measured at the closing rate. That program is F.05 — foreign currency valuation (in S/4HANA also available as the Fiori-scheduled job / FAGL_FCV).

Key term · Foreign currency valuation (F.05)

F.05 revalues (a) open items in AP/AR foreign currency and (b) G/L balances in foreign currency (e.g. a USD bank account) at the period-end exchange rate. The difference goes to an exchange gain/loss P&L account, with the offset on a balance sheet adjustment account — the original item keeps its original amount. Crucially, valuation postings are made with an automatic reversal date (first day of the next period): they exist only so the balance sheet date tells the truth; next period, business continues at the original rates until the item is really paid (when the realised gain/loss is posted). F.05 = mahina-akhir ki rate par dobara napna; posting agle period ke pehle din khud reverse ho jati hai.

Exam trap · Unrealised vs realised

F.05 books only unrealised differences (paper gains/losses on still-open items) and reverses them. The realised difference is posted later, at actual payment/clearing, from the real rates — and is never reversed. Mixing these two is the classic F.05 exam question. F.05 = kagzi (unrealised) farq, reverse hota hai; asal farq payment par banta hai, reverse nahi hota.

19 Balance carryforward — FAGLGVTR

The bridge from the old year to the new: balances walk across, P&L is emptied into retained earnings.

Key term · Balance carryforward (FAGLGVTR)

Balance carryforward is the year-end program — transaction FAGLGVTR for the G/L — that opens the new fiscal year: balance sheet accounts carry their closing balance forward as the new year's opening balance; P&L accounts are closed to zero and their total result is carried into the retained earnings account (the OB53 account from Part 2 · Lesson 2). Customer/vendor balances are carried forward with F.07, and assets have their own year-end closing (AJAB) and fiscal-year change (AJRW). The program can be re-run — later closing adjustments simply update the carried-forward amounts. Carryforward = purane saal ke balances naye saal ke opening balances bana dena; P&L ka result retained earnings mein chala jata hai.

Exam trap · Carryforward posts no document

Carryforward writes carried-forward balances into the new year — there is no journal document to reverse. If a late adjustment changes the old year, you simply run FAGLGVTR again and the opening balances update. Document nahi banta; dobara run karne se opening balances theek ho jate hain.

20 Financial Statement Version — OB58 & F.01, and Fiori apps for closing

The final product of all this work: the balance sheet and P&L, shaped by the FSV — and scheduled from Fiori.

Key term · Financial Statement Version (FSV)

A Financial Statement Version is the tree-shaped layout that turns G/L accounts into the published statements: which accounts sit under “Current Assets”, which under “Equity”, the headings, subtotals and the order of lines. It is defined in OB58 and assigned to the company code. The accounts hold the numbers; the FSV decides the shape of the report. FSV = balance sheet / P&L ki tarteeb aur headings — kaunsa account kis khane mein dikhega.

To produce the statements you run F.01 (the classic financial statement report — in reports also S_ALR_87012284): choose company code, FSV, ledger and the periods to compare. F.01 reads the balances and lays them out exactly as the FSV tree commands. A classic closing check: F.01 shows an “accounts not assigned” warning if a G/L account with a balance was never placed in the FSV — those amounts would silently miss the statements, so the closing team always checks this list.

Key term · SAP Fiori apps for closing

SAP Fiori is SAP's modern web-style interface of tile apps (Part 1). For closing, key Fiori apps include Manage Posting Periods (the Fiori face of OB52), Schedule General Ledger Jobs (runs F.05 valuation, carryforward and other closing jobs as scheduled background jobs with logs), Post General Journal Entries, Display Line Items in General Ledger, and the Advanced Financial Closing (SAP AFC) app family, which organises the whole closing as a task list with owners, deadlines and dependencies across company codes. The exam expects you to know closing is increasingly scheduled and monitored from Fiori, while the underlying programs stay the same. Fiori apps = closing ke kaam tiles ki shakal mein schedule aur monitor karna; andar program wohi purane hain.

21 Exam traps

The eight traps this lesson's topics set again and again in C_TS4FI.

Trap 1 · Chart of depreciation vs chart of accounts

Different objects. Chart of depreciation = depreciation areas per country, one per company code. Chart of accounts = G/L account list (OB13). Naam ki mushabihat hi jaal hai.

Trap 2 · AS01 posts nothing

Master creation = value zero. Value arrives only with an acquisition (F-90 / ABSO / MIRO route). File ≠ paisa.

Trap 3 · Depreciation is a run, not a journal

AFAB calculates and posts summarised; a failed run continues with a restart run. Manual FB50 depreciation breaks subledger reconciliation. AFAB hi sahi rasta hai.

Trap 4 · AuC is not depreciated

Assets under Construction collect costs with no depreciation until settled to the final asset. Ban raha asset purana nahi gina jata.

Trap 5 · FI12 house bank ≠ FI01 bank master

FI12 = our banks, per company code. FI01 = the general bank directory. House bank + account ID → G/L account is the chain payments use. House bank hamara bank hai.

Trap 6 · F.05 reverses; realised differences do not

Valuation = unrealised, auto-reversed next period. Realised gain/loss happens at payment and stays. Kagzi farq ulta ho jata hai, asal farq nahi.

Trap 7 · Carryforward makes no document

FAGLGVTR carries balances, P&L result goes to retained earnings (OB53). Re-run it after late adjustments — never “reverse” it. Document nahi, balance aage barhta hai.

Trap 8 · The FSV changes shape, never numbers

OB58 only arranges accounts into statement lines. An unassigned account with a balance is a closing error F.01 warns about. Tarteeb badalti hai, raqam nahi.

23 Summary — the whole lesson on one screen

If you remember this screen, you remember the lesson.

  • FI-AA is a subledger per asset, tied to the G/L by class account determination. NBV = APC − accumulated depreciation.
  • Chart of depreciation = areas (01 book posts to G/L; tax/group areas value in parallel). Asset class (OAOA) = template: accounts, number range, depreciation defaults. AuC is never depreciated.
  • Life cycle: AS01 master (value 0) → F-90 acquisition (31/70, type 100; ABSO for other cases) → ABUM transfer → AFAB monthly depreciation run → ABAVN scrap / F-92·ABAON sale → watch it all in AW01N.
  • Bank: house bank + account ID → G/L (FI12). F-58 pays with cheque into a clearing account; FF67 statement confirms; F-03 clears into the main bank account.

  • Closing: OB52 opens/closes periods → F.05 FX valuation (unrealised, auto-reverses) → FAGLGVTR carryforward (P&L → retained earnings) → statements via OB58 FSV + F.01; closing jobs scheduled and monitored in Fiori.
